Flat Roofing Done Right
the First Time

Flat and low-slope roofing systems are among the most common in the Lower Mainland — and among the most commonly installed incorrectly. Proper drainage, correct membrane selection, and watertight terminations are critical to a flat roof that performs.

Stonnex installs torch-on modified bitumen, TPO, and EPDM membrane systems for residential additions, garages, suites, strata buildings, and light commercial applications.

  • Torch-on modified bitumen (SBS and APP)
  • TPO single-ply membrane
  • EPDM rubber membrane
  • Drainage assessment and re-sloping where needed
  • Parapet wall and edge detail waterproofing

How we handle open-flame roofing

Torch-applied membrane is the right system for most flat and low-slope roofs in this climate. It also involves an open flame on a building, and it is the single thing in roofing that deserves the most discipline.

  • Every torch application is completed under a written hot works procedure.
  • A dedicated fire watch is maintained during application and for a set period after the last torch is shut down, as required by our hot works procedure and our insurer.
  • Extinguishers and a charged water source are on the roof before the first torch is lit.
  • Combustible materials are cleared from the work zone and adjacent penetrations are inspected before application begins.
  • The procedure is a contractual requirement for every installation partner, not a preference.

Flat and torch-on systems carry a 5-year Stonnex labour warranty. Manufacturer membrane warranties run 20 to 30 years depending on the system specified. Full terms and exclusions are on our warranty page.

Flat Roof Systems

Choosing the Right
Membrane System

Torch-On Modified Bitumen

The most widely used system in BC residential construction. SBS and APP modified bitumen membranes are applied with a propane torch for a seamless, durable bond. 15–25 year lifespan.

TPO Single-Ply

Thermoplastic polyolefin membrane is heat-welded at seams for a continuous waterproof surface. Excellent for larger flat roofs and commercial applications. Energy-efficient white option available.

EPDM Rubber

Durable synthetic rubber membrane suited to extreme temperature cycling. Common on residential additions and garages. Fully adhered or mechanically fastened installation options.

Common Questions

Flat Roofing
FAQs

How long does a flat roof last?
A properly installed torch-on modified bitumen roof lasts 15–25 years in BC conditions. TPO and EPDM systems can last 20–30 years with proper maintenance. Ponding water and blocked drains are the primary factors that shorten flat roof lifespan.
Can you reroof over an existing flat roof?
In some cases yes, but only if the existing membrane is in adequate condition and the deck is sound. In most cases we recommend removing the old membrane to inspect the decking and ensure proper drainage before installing the new system.
What's the difference between torch-on and TPO?
Torch-on is a multi-layer bitumen system applied with heat — it's well-suited to BC's wet climate and is the most common residential flat roofing choice. TPO is a single-ply membrane heat-welded at seams — better suited to larger commercial roofs where long seam runs make TPO's installation economics more favourable.
My flat roof is leaking. How quickly can you respond?
We prioritize active leaks. Call us at 604-935-6976 and we'll schedule an inspection as quickly as possible — typically within 24–48 hours.
Built for Rain

What Makes a Flat Roof
Last on the Wet Coast

Flat and low-slope roofs fail differently from pitched roofs. Water does not shed in minutes; it sits against every seam and detail for hours during a multi-day rain event. Four things decide how long a flat roof lasts here.

1. Slope to drain

A flat roof needs a minimum 2 percent slope to its drains. Where an older roof ponds, we build slope with tapered insulation during the replacement instead of installing new membrane over the same low spot. Water that clears within 48 hours of rain is the target.

2. Fully adhered, two-ply membrane

Our standard system is two-ply SBS: an IKO TP-180 base sheet and a TP-250 granulated cap sheet, both fully torched, so there are no fasteners through the waterproofing layer and no seam that depends on adhesive alone. The detail of how it is built is on the torch-on roofing page.

3. Details before field

Most flat-roof leaks start at a drain, a curb, a wall or a parapet, not in the open field of the roof. Membrane flashings go in at every detail before the cap sheet, and metal cap and counter flashings finish the edges.

4. Drains and overflows

New drains or scuppers, sized for the roof area, with an overflow outlet so a blocked drain cannot turn the roof into a pond. Clearing drains each fall is the single most valuable maintenance task on any flat roof.
