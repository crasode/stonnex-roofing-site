Roof Replacement Cost in Vancouver in 2026: What Homeowners Actually Pay

Published July 2, 2026 · Stonnex Roofing

Most homeowners searching this already know their roof needs attention. What they want is a real number. So here it is: a full roof replacement in Greater Vancouver typically runs between $9,000 and $30,000+, depending on your home’s size, the material you choose, and a handful of site-specific factors. This article breaks it down material by material, so you can walk into any quote conversation knowing what’s reasonable.

The Price Ranges by Material: A Straight Comparison

The biggest single variable in your roofing cost is the material. Here’s how the four main options stack up across the Lower Mainland and Fraser Valley.

Architectural Asphalt Shingles: The Most Common Choice in BC

Architectural shingles — sometimes called laminate or dimensional shingles — are the dominant choice across Surrey, Burnaby, Coquitlam, and most of the Fraser Valley. IKO, GAF, and Malarkey all manufacture BC-appropriate grades, and when installed correctly, they hold up well in our wet coastal climate.

For a typical 1,800 sq ft bungalow, expect to pay $10,500 to $14,000 installed. A two-storey home in the 2,500 sq ft range runs closer to $13,000 to $16,000. Those numbers include tear-off of your existing roof, new underlayment, ice-and-water shield at the eaves and valleys, and disposal.

Warranty depth varies by product line. IKO, GAF, and Malarkey all offer manufacturer warranties up to 50 years on their premium lines — but what you actually receive depends on the specific shingle and whether your contractor installs to manufacturer requirements.

Best for: Homeowners who want a reliable, well-supported product at a predictable price, with strong warranty backing.

Cedar Shake: Higher Cost, Higher Maintenance

Cedar shake roofs are common in North Vancouver, West Vancouver, and older neighbourhoods across the Lower Mainland. They look excellent and perform well — but they require active moss management in our wet coastal climate. A cedar roof that hasn’t been treated or cleaned regularly can lose years off its lifespan.

Replacement costs run $18,000 to $30,000 for most homes, and larger or more complex roofs go higher. The material itself costs more, installation is more labour-intensive, and tearing off an old shake roof adds time and disposal cost.

If you’re replacing cedar with cedar, budget for a deck inspection too. Older shake roofs sometimes hide rot in the decking underneath, and replacing damaged decking adds cost that’s hard to predict before the old roof comes off.

Best for: Homeowners in heritage or character neighbourhoods who want to maintain the original aesthetic and are prepared to keep up with maintenance over time.

Standing Seam Metal: The Long-Term Option

Metal roofing has grown in popularity across Greater Vancouver and the Fraser Valley, and it makes sense for this climate. It sheds moss and water beautifully — a real advantage here — requires almost no maintenance, and the best systems carry 50-year warranties. The trade-off is higher up-front cost.

Standing seam metal typically runs $22,000 to $40,000+ installed on a standard detached home. The wide range reflects panel profile, gauge, coating, and roof complexity. A simple gable roof costs less than a hip roof with dormers and valleys.

If you plan to stay in your home for 20 or more years, the math often works in metal’s favour. You’re unlikely to replace it again, and you eliminate most of the maintenance costs that accumulate with asphalt or cedar over the same period.

Best for: Long-term homeowners, those on heavily treed or moss-prone lots, and anyone who wants to replace their roof once and not think about it again.

Flat Membrane Systems: Coach Houses, Suites, and Modern Architecture

Flat and low-slope roofs are common on coach houses, secondary suites, and modern West Coast architecture throughout Vancouver, Richmond, and newer developments across the Lower Mainland. Torch-on SBS membrane — styrene-butadiene-styrene, a modified bitumen product — is the standard in BC for good reason. It bonds directly to the deck, handles our freeze-thaw cycles, and holds up under standing water far better than traditional built-up roofing.

Flat roof replacement typically runs $8,000 to $18,000, depending on the size of the roof, the number of drains, and whether insulation needs to be added or replaced. TPO (thermoplastic polyolefin) membrane is an alternative some homeowners choose for energy performance, particularly on larger flat sections.

Best for: Coach houses, secondary suites, flat-roofed additions, and any low-slope section where membrane is the appropriate system.

What Drives Costs Up (and Down) in BC

The material is only part of the story. These site-specific factors move the final number in either direction.

Factors that increase cost:

  • Steep pitch (above 8/12) requires additional safety equipment and slows installation
  • Multiple roof layers requiring full tear-off rather than overlay
  • Rotted or damaged decking discovered during tear-off
  • Complex geometry: dormers, valleys, skylights, chimneys, and multiple penetrations
  • Accessibility issues — tight lots, no staging area, proximity to power lines
  • Distance from the Lower Mainland core into the Fraser Valley can affect labour rates slightly

Factors that reduce cost:

  • Simple gable or hip roof with minimal penetrations
  • Single existing layer with solid decking underneath
  • Good site access for staging and equipment
  • Replacing asphalt with asphalt, same profile, no structural changes

One thing worth knowing: the cost of doing nothing compounds. A minor repair at $400 to $800 can prevent a water intrusion that damages insulation, framing, and drywall. If you’re not sure whether your roof needs repair or full replacement, that’s exactly what a professional inspection is for. And if you’re seeing signs of wear but aren’t certain how serious it is, this guide to signs you need a new roof is a useful starting point.

How Vancouver’s Climate Affects Your Material Choice

Greater Vancouver receives over 1,200mm of rain annually. Add coastal humidity, salt air near the water, and the moss growth that comes with shade and moisture — and you have a climate that’s harder on roofing than most of Canada.

Moss is the most underestimated cost driver here. It holds moisture against the roof surface, accelerates granule loss on asphalt shingles, and lifts cedar shakes over time. Metal roofing resists moss naturally. Asphalt and cedar both benefit from zinc or copper strips at the ridge and from periodic treatment.

FAQs: Roof Replacement Cost in Vancouver

How much does a roof replacement cost in Vancouver in 2026?

Most homeowners in Greater Vancouver pay between $9,000 and $30,000 for a full roof replacement, depending on the material, home size, and roof complexity. Architectural asphalt shingles typically run $9,000 to $16,000 installed. Cedar shake runs $18,000 to $30,000 or more. Standing seam metal starts around $22,000. Flat membrane systems for coach houses and suites typically range from $8,000 to $18,000.

What is the cheapest roofing option in BC?

Architectural asphalt shingles are the most affordable installed option for most detached homes. Products from IKO, GAF, and Malarkey perform well in BC’s wet coastal climate and carry manufacturer warranties up to 50 years on premium lines.

Does a steep roof cost more to replace?

Yes. Pitches above 8/12 require additional safety equipment and slow the installation process. Expect a steeper pitch to add 10 to 20 percent or more to the base cost, depending on how extreme the slope is.

How long does a new roof last in the Lower Mainland?

Architectural asphalt shingles typically last 25 to 30 years in BC’s climate with proper installation and basic maintenance. Cedar shake lasts 30 to 40 years with active moss management. Standing seam metal can last 50 years or more. Flat membrane systems typically last 20 to 30 years depending on the product and drainage quality.

Can I get a roof replacement quote without a sales visit?

Yes. Stonnex’s instant online quote tool generates real pricing from IKO, GAF, and Malarkey data using your address — takes about 60 seconds, no phone call required. If you’d prefer a written assessment, the free in-person inspection produces a report with photographs at no cost or obligation.

Why do roofing quotes vary so much in Greater Vancouver?

Because the variables are real. Roof size, pitch, number of layers, decking condition, material choice, and site accessibility all affect the final number. Contractors who quote low without seeing the roof often adjust after tear-off when hidden damage appears. A written inspection report before you commit helps avoid that surprise.

Does the Fraser Valley cost less for roof replacement than Vancouver?

Labour rates can vary slightly across the region, but the difference is generally modest. Material costs are consistent across the Lower Mainland and Fraser Valley — the same manufacturer pricing applies throughout. The bigger cost drivers are always the roof itself: size, pitch, complexity, and condition.
