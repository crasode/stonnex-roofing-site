Roof Repair in Burnaby: What Homeowners Pay and What to Expect

Published August 28, 2026 · Stonnex Roofing

Burnaby's housing stock skews older, the wet coastal climate is relentless, and a small problem left alone through one rainy season has a way of becoming a much larger one. This article covers what drives repair costs, what different repair types actually involve, how to decide between repair and replacement, and what to expect when you hire a licensed contractor.

Why Burnaby roofs need attention sooner than most

Burnaby sits in one of the wettest urban climates in Canada. Annual rainfall in Metro Vancouver regularly exceeds 1,100 mm, and Burnaby's hillside neighbourhoods — from Burnaby Mountain to the slopes above Metrotown — catch extra moisture from fog and low cloud on top of that.

That sustained exposure does specific things to a roof:

  • Moss and algae colonise asphalt shingles, lifting their edges and accelerating granule loss
  • Freeze-thaw cycles at higher elevations crack caulking around flashings and skylights
  • Ice damming on north-facing slopes forces water under shingles during colder winters
  • Cedar shake absorbs moisture, splits, and curls faster without regular maintenance

Most Burnaby homes built between the 1960s and early 2000s are on their second or third roof. If yours is 15 years or older and hasn't had a recent inspection, minor issues are likely already developing.

What affects the cost of roof repair in Burnaby

Every job is priced individually because no two roofs are the same. That said, the variables that move the number are consistent.

Roof size and pitch

Steeper roofs cost more to work on. Contractors need additional safety equipment, take longer to move across the surface, and carry higher risk. A low-slope bungalow in South Burnaby is a fundamentally different job from a steep gabled home on the Burnaby Mountain slopes.

Type and extent of damage

A single cracked flashing around a chimney is a contained repair. A section of shingles that's been leaking for two seasons often means hidden rot in the decking underneath — and that rot has to come out before anything new goes on top. That adds both material and labour to the scope.

Roofing material

Architectural asphalt is the most common material in Burnaby and the most straightforward to repair. Cedar shake requires matching the existing profile and sourcing compatible material. Standing-seam metal and slate are more specialised and take longer. Whatever's already on your roof sets the baseline cost.

Access and site conditions

Mature trees overhanging the roof, tight lot lines, or steep driveways all add time and equipment. In some Burnaby neighbourhoods — Burnaby Heights and Capitol Hill in particular — lot access is genuinely constrained.

Flashings and penetrations

Flashings around chimneys, skylights, vents, and valleys are the most common source of leaks on roofs that are otherwise in reasonable shape. Reflashing a chimney or skylight is a defined scope of work, but if the underlying structure has deteriorated, the cost goes up.

Common repair types and what they involve

Shingle replacement

Replacing a section of damaged or missing shingles is one of the most frequent repairs on Burnaby homes. The work involves removing the damaged material, checking the decking below for soft spots or rot, and installing new shingles with proper underlayment. If the decking is sound, it's a contained job.

Flashing repair and replacement

Flashings are the metal strips that seal joints between your roof and any vertical surface — chimneys, dormers, skylights, vent pipes. They're the most common failure point on roofs over 10 years old. When the shingles themselves are still in decent shape, flashing repair is often the right fix.

Flat roof membrane repair

Many Burnaby coach houses, secondary suites, and newer homes have flat or low-slope sections covered in torch-on membrane or similar systems. Membrane repairs involve identifying the breach, cutting back to sound material, and applying a compatible patch. Done properly, this extends the membrane's life significantly.

Gutter and drainage repairs

Gutters aren't technically part of the roof, but failed gutters cause roof damage. Water backing up under fascia boards, overflowing against siding, and pooling at the foundation all trace back to drainage problems. Seamless aluminum gutters sized for BC rainfall volumes are the standard fix.

Decking replacement

When a leak has been active for a while, the plywood or OSB decking beneath the shingles absorbs moisture and deteriorates. Soft spots underfoot, or visible sagging from inside the attic, indicate decking damage. Once the rot is present, replacing it is not optional — it has to go before anything new is installed.

Repair vs. replacement: how to think about it

This is the question most Burnaby homeowners are actually trying to answer. Honestly, it depends on the age of the roof, the extent of the damage, and what the inspection finds.

A few useful reference points:

  • Architectural asphalt shingles typically last 20 to 30 years in our wet coastal climate. If your roof is past 20 years and showing widespread granule loss, curling, or multiple leak points, repair is often throwing money at a surface that needs replacing.
  • Cedar shake has a similar lifespan but degrades faster without maintenance. Widespread splitting and moss colonisation across the whole surface is a replacement signal, not a repair one.
  • Metal and slate last considerably longer, and targeted repairs on these materials make sense well into the roof's life.

Isolated damage on a roof that's otherwise in good shape? Repair is the right call. If the inspection reveals widespread deterioration, you're better off understanding the full replacement cost before committing to patchwork. Signs the damage is worse than it looks

Some roof problems are visible from the ground. Others only show up once you're up there or inside the attic. These are worth knowing about before you get a quote:

  • Daylight visible from the attic means there's a breach somewhere
  • Dark staining on attic sheathing indicates moisture has been entering for some time
  • Sagging or soft areas on the roof surface suggest decking deterioration below
  • Granules accumulating in gutters after rain signal shingle wear across a wider area than the visible damage
  • Interior water stains that appear away from the roof edge often trace to flashing failures, not shingle damage

If any of these apply to your Burnaby home, the signs you need a new roof article covers the full list of indicators that separate a repair situation from a replacement one.

What to look for in a Burnaby roofing contractor

Burnaby falls under the City of Burnaby's building permit requirements, and roofing work above a certain scope requires a permit. A licensed contractor handles this as part of the job. Beyond licensing, a few things matter when you're choosing who to hire.

WorkSafeBC registration is not optional. If a worker is injured on your property and the contractor isn't registered, you can be held liable. Verify it before anyone gets on your roof.

BBB Accredited Business status gives you a dispute resolution path if something goes wrong. It's a meaningful signal, not just a logo.

Written documentation at every stage — inspection report, scope of work, warranty terms. Verbal agreements don't hold up when there's a disagreement about what was included.

Named accountability on the job. Knowing who your project manager is and being able to reach them directly is worth asking about when you're comparing contractors.

Frequently asked questions

How much does roof repair cost in Burnaby?

Pricing varies by repair type, roof material, extent of damage, and site conditions. Every job is different, which is why Stonnex doesn't publish flat-rate pricing. You can get a real estimate from your address using the instant quote tool at Stonnex.com, or book a free in-person inspection for a fully scoped written quote.

Does roof repair in Burnaby require a building permit?

Minor repairs — replacing a section of shingles or reflashing a chimney — typically don't require a permit. Larger scope work, including significant decking replacement or structural changes, may. A licensed contractor will advise you on permit requirements for your specific job and handle the application if needed.

How do I know if I need a repair or a full replacement?

Age and extent of damage are the two main factors. A roof past 20 years with widespread granule loss, multiple leak points, or significant decking deterioration is often a replacement candidate. An isolated flashing failure or a small section of damaged shingles on an otherwise sound roof is a repair. A written inspection report gives you the information to make that call without guessing.

Can I claim roof repair on my home insurance in BC?

It depends on the cause. Sudden damage from a storm or falling tree is typically covered. Gradual deterioration from age or lack of maintenance generally isn't. A written inspection report from a licensed contractor documenting the cause of damage strengthens an insurance claim. Stonnex inspection reports are accepted by major BC insurers and lenders.

How long does a roof repair take in Burnaby?

Most residential repairs are completed in a single day. More complex jobs involving decking replacement, multiple damage areas, or flat membrane work may take two days. Your project manager will give you a clear timeline before work starts.

What causes most roof leaks in Burnaby homes?

Failed or deteriorated flashings around chimneys, skylights, and vents are the most common cause on roofs that are otherwise in reasonable condition. On older roofs, widespread shingle wear, moss damage, and cracked underlayment are the typical culprits. In Burnaby's hillside neighbourhoods, ice damming on north-facing slopes causes leaks that are often misdiagnosed as flashing failures.

Is it worth repairing a roof that's 20 years old?

It depends on what the inspection finds. If the damage is isolated and the rest of the roof is structurally sound, a targeted repair extends its life at a fraction of replacement cost. If the inspection reveals widespread deterioration, you're patching a surface that needs replacing. A written inspection report gives you the honest picture so you can make an informed decision.
