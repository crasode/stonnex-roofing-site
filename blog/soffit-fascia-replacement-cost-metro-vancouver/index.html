Soffit and Fascia Replacement Cost in Metro Vancouver: 2026 Guide

Published August 21, 2026 · Stonnex Roofing

If you’re getting quotes for soffit and fascia work in Metro Vancouver or the Fraser Valley, the spread in numbers can be surprising. That’s not a red flag — it reflects real differences in materials, linear footage, and what’s hiding underneath. This guide breaks down what drives the cost in 2026, what to watch for on a typical Lower Mainland home, and how to get a number that actually applies to your property.

What soffit and fascia actually do

The fascia is the horizontal board running along the edge of your roofline, directly behind the gutters. The soffit is the underside of the roof overhang — the part you see when you look up at your eaves from outside.

Together they seal the gap between your roof deck and the exterior wall, keeping rain, insects, and birds out of your attic. They also allow ventilation to move through the eaves so moisture doesn’t build up inside the roof structure.

On the coast, where Metro Vancouver receives well over 1,200mm of rain annually, that ventilation and moisture barrier function is not optional. Blocked or rotted soffits are one of the more common contributors to premature roof failure in older homes across Burnaby, North Vancouver, and Langley.

Why soffit and fascia replacement costs vary

There’s no flat rate for this work. What you’ll pay depends on several overlapping factors.

Linear footage

Soffit and fascia are priced by the linear foot, not by square footage. A modest bungalow in Surrey might have 120 linear feet of fascia. A two-storey home in West Vancouver with deep overhangs could have 300 or more. More linear footage means more material and more time on the job.

Material choice

The three most common materials in Metro Vancouver right now:

  • Vinyl — lower material cost, reasonable durability, but can crack in hard freezes and won’t hold paint if you want to change the colour later
  • Aluminum — the most common choice on the coast; handles moisture well, holds paint, and doesn’t rot
  • Wood (finger-jointed or clear cedar) — higher cost, requires painting and periodic maintenance, but suits heritage homes in areas like Kerrisdale or older neighbourhoods in New Westminster where original character matters

Aluminum is generally the right choice here for most Lower Mainland homes. It performs well in our wet coastal climate and needs minimal upkeep.

Condition of the existing structure

If the fascia board has rotted through to the rafter tails or the decking behind it, that has to be addressed before new soffit and fascia go on. This turns up regularly on homes built in the 1980s and 1990s across Coquitlam, Maple Ridge, and Abbotsford. Rot repair adds to the total, but skipping it means the new material won’t hold properly.

Accessibility and roof pitch

Single-storey homes are straightforward. A steep-pitch two-storey in North Vancouver or a home with complex gable ends takes longer to set up safely and adds labour hours overall.

Whether gutters are included

Soffit and fascia replacement often coincides with gutter replacement, since the gutters attach directly to the fascia. Doing both at the same time lowers the combined mobilisation cost compared to scheduling them separately. Stonnex installs seamless aluminum gutters and can coordinate both scopes under one project manager.

Signs the work is overdue

Soffit and fascia damage isn’t always obvious until it’s been there a while. Watch for:

  • Peeling or bubbling paint on the fascia boards
  • Soft spots or visible rot when you press on the fascia
  • Gaps or sagging in the soffit panels
  • Birds or insects getting into the eaves
  • Water stains on the exterior wall below the roofline
  • Moss growing along the eave line — a reliable sign of trapped moisture

If you’re already seeing signs you need a new roof — curling shingles, granule loss, interior water staining — there’s a reasonable chance the soffit and fascia are also due. Eave damage and roof surface damage tend to develop together.

Combining soffit and fascia work with a roof replacement

If your home is 20 or more years old and you’re already replacing the roof, have the soffit and fascia assessed at the same time. Replacing them as part of the same project reduces total labour cost and avoids disturbing a new roof later to access the eave structure.

The best roofing material for the BC coast depends on your budget, roof pitch, and how long you plan to stay. Architectural asphalt shingles, standing-seam metal, and cedar shake all have different eave and ventilation requirements — the soffit spec should match whichever roofing system you choose.

What to ask a contractor before signing

Before committing to any soffit and fascia quote in Metro Vancouver, ask:

  • Is this price based on a site visit, or a phone estimate?
  • Does the quote include rot repair if it turns up during installation?
  • Who is the named contact managing my project day to day?
  • Are you WorkSafeBC registered?
  • What warranty applies to the materials and the installation?

FAQs

How much does soffit and fascia replacement cost in Metro Vancouver in 2026?

Pricing varies by linear footage, material choice, accessibility, and whether rot repair is needed. There’s no reliable flat rate. The most accurate way to get a number for your home is a site inspection, which Stonnex provides free with a written report.

How long does soffit and fascia replacement take?

Most single-family homes in the Lower Mainland take one to two days. Larger homes, complex rooflines, or significant rot repair can extend that.

Can I replace just the fascia and leave the soffit?

Sometimes — but it depends on the condition of both. If the soffit is original to a 30-year-old home and the fascia has rotted, the soffit panels are likely showing wear too. Replacing one without the other can create a visual mismatch and may not resolve the underlying ventilation issues.

Does soffit and fascia replacement affect my roof warranty?

It can, depending on how the work is done. Improper ventilation at the eaves affects attic airflow, which affects shingle performance and manufacturer warranty validity. Work done by a certified contractor who understands the full roofing system avoids that risk.

Should I replace gutters at the same time?

If your gutters are more than 15 years old or are attached to fascia that’s being replaced, doing both together is generally more cost-effective. The labour overlap is significant since the gutters attach directly to the fascia.

How do I know if my soffit has ventilation problems?

Common signs include excessive attic heat in summer, ice damming along the eaves in winter (less common in Metro Vancouver, but it does happen), and moisture or mould in the attic space. A written inspection report from a certified contractor will document ventilation condition specifically.
