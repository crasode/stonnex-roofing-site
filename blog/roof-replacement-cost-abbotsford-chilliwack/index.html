How Much Does a New Roof Cost in Abbotsford and Chilliwack in 2026?

Published July 10, 2026 · Stonnex Roofing

If you own a home in Abbotsford or Chilliwack and you’re starting to think about a roof replacement, the first question is almost always the same: what’s this actually going to cost me? It’s a fair question, and it deserves a straight answer.

The honest reality is that costs vary quite a bit — your home’s size, the material you choose, the roof pitch, and what the crew finds once the old shingles come off all play a role. This article walks through real numbers for the Fraser Valley’s eastern corridor in 2026, explains what drives costs up or down, and shows you the fastest way to get a figure specific to your address.

What Roof Replacements Actually Cost in Abbotsford and Chilliwack

For most detached homes in Abbotsford and Chilliwack, a full roof replacement using architectural asphalt shingles runs somewhere between $9,000 and $18,000. That range covers the majority of single-family homes in the area.

Smaller ranchers and bungalows sit toward the lower end. Larger two-storey homes with complex rooflines push toward the upper end. Cedar shake and metal go higher still — more on that below.

These figures reflect 2026 material and labour costs in the Fraser Valley. They’re not Metro Vancouver prices, which tend to run slightly higher due to demand and contractor travel time.

Material Costs: What You’re Choosing Between

The material you select is the single biggest variable in your final quote. Here’s an honest look at each option.

Architectural Asphalt Shingles

Architectural shingles — sometimes called laminate or dimensional shingles — are the most common choice in the Fraser Valley. Brands like IKO, GAF, and Malarkey make shingles built for wet coastal climates, with algae-resistant granules and SBS-modified cores that handle the freeze-thaw cycles Abbotsford and Chilliwack see more often than the coast does.

Typical installed cost: $9,000 to $18,000 for an average home. Lifespan: 25 to 35 years with proper ventilation. Best for homeowners who want reliable performance, a wide colour selection, and manufacturer warranties up to 50 years — without paying the premium for metal or slate.

Cedar Shake

Cedar shake is still popular in older Abbotsford and Chilliwack neighbourhoods, and it looks exceptional on the right home. The trade-off is maintenance. In our wet coastal climate, cedar needs moss management every few years, and it’s more vulnerable to moisture damage if attic ventilation is inadequate.

Typical installed cost: $18,000 to $35,000 depending on home size and shake grade. Lifespan: 20 to 30 years with active maintenance. Best for homeowners replacing an existing cedar roof who want to preserve the look and understand the ongoing upkeep involved.

Standing Seam Metal

Metal is the longest-lasting option and sheds moss and water beautifully — a real advantage in the Fraser Valley, where annual rainfall regularly exceeds 1,200mm and moss colonises neglected roofs within a few seasons. The trade-off is higher up-front cost.

Typical installed cost: $25,000 to $50,000 or more depending on roof complexity. Lifespan: 40 to 70 years. Best for homeowners planning to stay long-term who want a roof they’ll likely never replace again.

Slate

Slate is the premium end of the market. It’s heavy, which means your home’s structure needs to support it, and installation requires a specialist. Not every contractor in the Fraser Valley works with it.

Typical installed cost: $35,000 and up. Lifespan: 75 to 100 years. Best for heritage homes or high-value properties where the aesthetic and longevity justify the cost.

What Pushes Your Quote Higher

Several factors can move a quote well above the base range. Knowing them ahead of time means no surprises when the estimate lands.

Roof Pitch

Steep roofs cost more to work on. Crews need additional safety equipment, move more slowly, and waste more material on cuts. If your home has a 9/12 pitch or steeper, expect a 15 to 25 percent premium over a standard 4/12 or 6/12 roof.

Roof Size and Complexity

Roofing is priced by the square — one square equals 100 square feet of roof surface. A simple gable roof on a 1,500 square foot bungalow might be 18 to 20 squares. A two-storey home with dormers, valleys, and hips can run 30 to 40 squares, with significantly more cutting and flashing work involved.

Decking Condition

Once the old shingles come off, the crew inspects the decking underneath. On Abbotsford and Chilliwack homes built in the 1980s and 1990s, rotted or delaminated plywood is common — especially around valleys and near chimneys. Replacing damaged decking is not optional if you want the new roof to last. Budget $60 to $100 per sheet if replacement is needed.

Number of Layers Being Removed

BC building code limits how many shingle layers a roof can carry. If your home already has two layers, both need to come off before a new roof goes on. A full tear-off of two layers adds labour time and disposal costs.

Flashings and Penetrations

Every chimney, skylight, pipe stack, and valley needs metal flashing. Old flashings are typically replaced during a full roof replacement. The more penetrations your home has, the more this adds to the total.

Labour in the Fraser Valley: What’s Different from Vancouver

Abbotsford and Chilliwack are the eastern anchors of the Fraser Valley, and roofing labour here is generally more competitive than in Metro Vancouver. Demand is somewhat lower, and contractors working this corridor don’t carry the same overhead as those based in Vancouver or Burnaby.

That said, workmanship quality varies considerably. The Fraser Valley has seen its share of unlicensed crews operating without WorkSafeBC coverage. Always verify that any contractor you hire is WorkSafeBC registered before work begins — it protects you if someone is injured on your property.

How Home Age Affects the Cost

Most Abbotsford and Chilliwack homes now due for a roof replacement were built between 1985 and 2005 — putting them 20 to 40 years old, right at or past the typical lifespan of the original asphalt shingles.

Homes in that age range often share a few cost-affecting traits: attic ventilation that doesn’t meet today’s standards, original flashings past their useful life, and sometimes no ice-and-water shield at all (it wasn’t always required). Correcting these during a replacement adds cost, but it prevents bigger problems down the road.

Abbotsford vs. Chilliwack: Is There a Price Difference?

In practice, the difference is minor. Both cities draw from the same Fraser Valley supply chain, so material costs are identical. Labour rates are comparable. The main variable is travel time — contractors based in Langley or further west sometimes pass on a modest surcharge. Those based in or near Abbotsford and Chilliwack typically don’t.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a roof replacement cost in Abbotsford in 2026?

Most detached homes in Abbotsford can expect to pay between $9,000 and $18,000 for architectural asphalt shingles, installed. Cedar shake runs $18,000 to $35,000, and standing seam metal starts around $25,000. The final number depends on your roof’s size, pitch, and the condition of the decking underneath.

Is roofing cheaper in Chilliwack than in Vancouver?

Generally, yes. Labour costs in Chilliwack and Abbotsford tend to be somewhat lower than in Metro Vancouver, and contractors based in the Fraser Valley don’t carry the same overhead as those operating out of Vancouver or Burnaby. The difference isn’t dramatic, but it’s real.

How long does a new roof last in the Fraser Valley?

Architectural asphalt shingles typically last 25 to 35 years in the Fraser Valley’s climate. Cedar shake lasts 20 to 30 years with regular maintenance. Standing seam metal can last 40 to 70 years. Proper attic ventilation is the biggest factor in whether any material reaches the upper end of its lifespan.

What’s included in a roof replacement quote?

A complete quote should cover tear-off and disposal of the existing material, new decking replacement where needed, ice-and-water shield along eaves and valleys, underlayment, the chosen roofing material, new flashings at all penetrations and transitions, and cleanup. Always confirm what’s included — and what isn’t — before signing anything.

Do I need a permit to replace my roof in Abbotsford or Chilliwack?

A straight replacement using the same material and roof profile typically doesn’t require a permit in most BC municipalities. Structural changes, adding skylights, or switching from a light material to something heavy like slate may require one. Your contractor should confirm this before work begins.

How do I know if I need a full replacement or just a repair?

If your roof is under 15 years old and the damage is isolated, a repair is often the right call. Past 20 years, with widespread granule loss, multiple leak points, or failing flashings, replacement usually makes more financial sense than patching. A written inspection report is the most reliable way to know for sure.
