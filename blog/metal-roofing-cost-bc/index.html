What a Metal Roof Costs in BC (2026-27)

Published 8 November 2026 · Stonnex Roofing

Metal is the material people ask about with the widest gap between expectation and quote. Some arrive expecting asphalt-plus-twenty-percent; others have been told a metal roof costs six figures. In the Lower Mainland, for most houses, the truth sits between those two, and where it sits depends mainly on which metal system you choose.

Here is what the numbers look like going into 2026-27, and what moves them.

The range

Across the Lower Mainland, professionally installed metal roofing currently runs between about $12 and $31 per square foot of roof area, before GST. That band covers three quite different systems:

SystemInstalled, per sq ftWhere it fits
Exposed-fastener panel (corrugated / ribbed)$12 – $18Workshops, outbuildings, budget-driven projects
Standing seam$15 – $28The standard residential choice; concealed fasteners
Metal tile / metal shingle$20 – $31Homes where the look of shake, slate or tile matters

On a typical detached home, that translates to a total project in the $30,000 to $60,000 range for standing seam, with compact simple roofs coming in under that and large or complex roofs going over it. A 2,000 square foot roof at $15 to $28 per square foot is $30,000 to $56,000 before tax, which matches what we see quoted across the region. Note that these figures describe roof area, not floor area: a steep roof over a 2,000 square foot house can easily be 2,600 square feet of roofing.

For context against other materials, our region-wide cost guide covers asphalt, and the composite shake guide covers Brava.

Standing seam vs metal tile

Standing seam

Long vertical panels, joined at raised seams, with the fasteners concealed under the seam. Nothing penetrates the weather surface, which is exactly what you want in a climate that delivers rain most of the year. It is the system most Lower Mainland homeowners mean when they say "metal roof," and the one we quote most often.

Within the $15 to $28 band, the spread comes from three choices. Snap-lock profiles in a standard-gauge coated steel sit at the lower end. Mechanically seamed panels, needed on low slopes, cost more to install. And the coating matters: a PVDF finish costs more than entry-level polyester but holds its colour and resists the coastal wet far better. Aluminum panels, worth considering close to salt water, carry a premium over steel.

Metal tile and metal shingle

Stamped panels that read as shake, slate or Mediterranean tile from the street. They suit character homes where a long vertical panel would look wrong, and strata or heritage contexts where the look has to be preserved. The trade-off is cost: more pieces, more accessories and more labour per square put the installed range at roughly $20 to $31 per square foot.

If the goal is specifically a cedar shake appearance, it is worth comparing metal tile against composite shake before deciding; our Brava comparison covers how composite performs here.

What actually moves the price

Roof complexity

Metal punishes complexity more than asphalt does. Every valley, hip, dormer and skylight on a metal roof means custom-formed flashing, and on standing seam each change of plane interrupts the panel runs. A simple gable roof and a West Coast contemporary with six planes can differ by thousands of dollars at the same area.

Tear-off

Removing the existing roof and disposing of it adds roughly $2 to $5 per square foot. Installing over the old layer is sometimes raised as a saving; for metal in this climate we do not recommend it, because it hides the deck condition and complicates flashing details that need to be right for fifty years.

Metal, gauge and coating

These three specification lines explain most of the difference between two standing seam quotes. Thicker gauge costs more. PVDF coatings cost more than polyester. Aluminum costs more than steel. None of this is visible from the street on day one; all of it shows up over the decades the roof is supposed to last. A quote that says only "metal roof, supplied and installed" has left the most important lines out.

Access and pitch

Steep pitches need more safety setup and slow the install. Tight urban lots with nowhere to stage long panels add handling time, since standing seam panels often arrive at the full length of the roof plane.

The lifespan arithmetic

The honest case for metal is not the purchase price, it is the number of roofs you buy. Asphalt shingle in the wet BC climate typically delivers 15 to 20 years. A quality metal roof here is generally expected to last 40 to 70 years or more, with the coating, not the metal, usually setting the limit.

Put plainly: a $45,000 metal roof over 50 years and a $18,000 asphalt roof replaced twice more over the same period end up closer than the day-one quotes suggest, before counting the disruption of each extra reroof. That arithmetic does not make metal right for every house or every ownership horizon, and if you plan to sell within a few years the maths often favours asphalt. But it is the comparison worth making, rather than quote against quote.

How to compare metal quotes

Totals alone tell you very little, because two metal quotes are rarely for the same system. Check that each quote states:

  • The system: exposed fastener, snap-lock standing seam, mechanically seamed, or tile profile
  • The metal and gauge, and the coating by name (polyester, SMP or PVDF)
  • Full tear-off to the deck, with the disposal included
  • Underlayment specification, since metal in this climate needs a proper high-temperature membrane, not just felt
  • Flashing: new custom-formed flashings at every penetration, not reused existing ones
  • Snow guards where roof planes discharge over doors and walkways
  • The rate for replacing rotten decking, agreed before it is found
  • The labour warranty, separately from the manufacturer finish warranty

That last distinction matters as much with metal as with any material. A 40-year finish warranty covers the coating; it does not cover the installation. Our warranty page sets out how the two differ.

British Columbia does not license roofing contractors, and metal is less forgiving of inexperience than asphalt. Ask every bidder for a WorkSafeBC clearance letter and proof of liability insurance, and ask directly how many standing seam roofs their own crew installed in the last year.

Common questions

How much does a metal roof cost in BC?

In the Lower Mainland, most professionally installed metal roofs land between about $12 and $31 per square foot of roof area, before GST. Exposed-fastener panels sit at the low end, standing seam in the middle to upper-middle, and metal tile or shingle profiles at the top. For an average detached home that usually means a total project in the $30,000 to $60,000 range.

Is standing seam or metal tile cheaper?

Standing seam is usually the less expensive of the two. A typical standing seam installation in the Lower Mainland runs roughly $15 to $28 per square foot installed, while metal tile and shingle profiles generally run about $20 to $31 because of the extra pieces, accessories and labour they involve.

Why is metal roofing so much more expensive than asphalt?

The material itself costs more, and the labour is specialist work: panels are cut and formed for your roof, and every penetration needs custom flashing. The offset is lifespan. A quality metal roof in coastal BC is generally expected to last 40 to 70 years or more, against 15 to 20 years for asphalt shingle in this climate, so many owners reroof once instead of two or three times.

Does the coastal climate change what metal I should choose?

Yes. Close to salt water, aluminum resists corrosion better than bare-edged steel, and on steel the coating matters: a PVDF finish holds up better in the wet Lower Mainland climate than entry-level polyester coatings. These choices move the price, so the quote should state the metal, the gauge and the coating.

Is GST charged on a metal roof?

Yes. Roofing in BC is subject to 5% GST on the contract price, and quotes are normally shown before tax. When comparing quotes, confirm each one treats GST the same way.
