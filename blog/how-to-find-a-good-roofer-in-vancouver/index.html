How to Find a Good Roofer in Vancouver: Questions to Ask Before You Sign

Published July 25, 2026 · Stonnex Roofing

Your roof is one of the most expensive parts of your home to replace — and in Vancouver’s wet climate, a bad installation or the wrong contractor can cost you far more than the original quote. Finding someone trustworthy takes more than a quick Google search. You need to know what to ask, what to look for, and when to walk away.

This guide covers exactly that.

Why Hiring the Right Roofer Matters More in Vancouver

The Lower Mainland receives over 1,100 mm of rain annually. That kind of sustained moisture punishes poor workmanship fast. Flashing that isn’t properly sealed, rushed underlayment, or shingles without adequate overlap won’t last decades — they’ll fail within a few seasons.

On top of that, unlicensed and out-of-province contractors regularly show up after storms looking for quick work. That makes doing your homework before signing anything genuinely important here, not just good practice.

Step 1: Verify They’re Licensed and Insured in BC

This is non-negotiable. In British Columbia, roofing contractors must be registered with WorkSafeBC and hold a valid business licence. Ask for both before the conversation goes any further.

You’ll also want to confirm they carry general liability insurance — typically a minimum of $2 million for residential work. If a worker is injured on your property and the contractor isn’t properly covered, you could be the one held liable.

Ask directly: “Can you provide your WorkSafeBC clearance letter and proof of liability insurance?” A legitimate contractor will have these ready without hesitation.

Step 2: Ask How Long They’ve Been Operating Locally

There’s a real difference between a contractor who has been roofing in Burnaby and Coquitlam for fifteen years and one who relocated from Alberta last spring. Local experience means familiarity with BC building codes, the specific demands of coastal weather, and the materials that actually hold up here.

Ask: “How long have you been working in the Lower Mainland specifically?” and “Do you have references from jobs in this area I can contact?”

Step 3: Get at Least Three Written Quotes

Verbal estimates are meaningless. Every quote should be written and should itemize the scope of work, materials, labour, disposal fees, and timeline. A lump sum with no breakdown is a problem.

Three quotes give you a realistic picture of market pricing. If you’re not sure whether your roof needs a full replacement or just repairs, reading up on signs you need a new roof before your quotes come in will help you evaluate what each contractor is actually proposing.

A quote that comes in dramatically lower than the others usually means something is being cut — cheaper materials, fewer layers of underlayment, or labour shortcuts you won’t notice until water starts coming in.

Step 4: Understand What Materials They’re Using

Not all shingles, membranes, or underlayments are equal. Ask what specific products the contractor plans to use and why. A good roofer can explain the difference between a standard 3-tab shingle and an architectural shingle, or why a torch-on membrane makes sense for a flat section of your roof.

Before your quotes arrive, the breakdown of roofing materials suited to the BC coast is worth reading. It’ll help you ask sharper questions and recognize when a contractor is speccing down without telling you.

Step 5: Ask About Warranties — Both Manufacturer and Workmanship

There are two separate warranties to understand. The manufacturer warranty covers the materials themselves, often ranging from 25 to 50 years depending on the product. The workmanship warranty covers the installation — and this is where many contractors fall short.

Ask: “What workmanship warranty do you offer, and is it in writing?” A five-year workmanship warranty is reasonable. Some contractors offer less, or nothing at all.

Also ask whether they’re a certified installer for the manufacturer whose products they’re using. Some manufacturer warranties are voided if the product isn’t installed by a certified contractor — a detail that matters a lot when you’re counting on coverage years down the road.

Step 6: Ask Who Will Actually Be on Your Roof

Some roofing companies win the contract and then subcontract the work to whoever is available that week. That’s not automatically a dealbreaker, but you deserve to know. Ask whether the crew is employed directly by the company, and whether a project manager will be on site or reachable throughout the job.

Having a named point of contact matters. If something goes wrong mid-project, you want one person accountable — not a chain of people pointing at each other.

Step 7: Watch for These Red Flags

A few patterns should give you pause regardless of how competitive the price looks:

  • Large upfront deposits. Ten to twenty percent is normal. Fifty percent or more before work starts is not.
  • No physical address or local presence. If a contractor can’t give you a local office address, be cautious.
  • Pressure to sign immediately. Any contractor who tells you the price expires in 24 hours is running a sales tactic, not offering a fair quote.
  • No mention of permits. Depending on the scope, a building permit may be required. A contractor who doesn’t raise this may be planning to skip it.
  • Cash only. This is often a sign the business isn’t operating above board.

Step 8: Check Reviews and Ask for References

Google reviews are useful, but they’re also easy to manipulate. Look for patterns across multiple reviews rather than individual five-star posts. Pay attention to how the company responds to negative feedback — that tells you more about how they handle problems than the glowing ones do.

Beyond reviews, ask for two or three references from recent jobs in your area. Actually call them. Ask whether the crew showed up on time, whether the final invoice matched the quote, and whether they’d hire the same contractor again.

What to Expect on Price

Roofing costs in Metro Vancouver run higher than most of Canada, driven by labour rates, material pricing, and the complexity of working in a dense urban environment. FAQs

How do I verify a roofer is licensed in BC?

Ask for their WorkSafeBC registration number and request a current clearance letter. You can also search the WorkSafeBC website directly to confirm the business is in good standing. A valid municipal business licence is a separate requirement.

What’s a reasonable deposit for a roofing job in Vancouver?

Ten to twenty percent of the total project cost is standard. Anything above that — especially requests for fifty percent or more before work begins — is worth taking seriously as a warning sign.

How many quotes should I get before hiring a roofer?

Three written, itemized quotes is the standard recommendation. It gives you enough data to spot outliers in both directions and helps you compare scope, not just price.

What questions should I ask about warranties?

Ask about both the manufacturer warranty on materials and the contractor’s workmanship warranty. Get both in writing. Also confirm whether the contractor is a certified installer for the brand they’re using, since some manufacturer warranties require certified installation to stay valid.

Is a building permit required for a roof replacement in Vancouver?

It depends on the scope of work and the municipality. In many cases, a full tear-off and replacement does require a permit. Ask your contractor directly, and be cautious if they suggest skipping it to save time or money.

What does it mean if a roofer subcontracts the work?

It means the crew doing the physical installation may not be direct employees of the company you hired. This isn’t automatically a problem, but ask upfront so you know who will be on your property and who is accountable if something goes wrong.

How long does a roof replacement typically take in Vancouver?

Most residential replacements take one to three days depending on the size, pitch, and complexity of the roof. Weather delays are common in the Lower Mainland, so a reliable contractor will build flexibility into the timeline rather than committing to a fixed completion date.

Taking the time to ask these questions before you sign protects your investment and your home. The right roofer will answer every one of them clearly and without hesitation.
