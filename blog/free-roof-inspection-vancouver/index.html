Free Roof Inspection in Vancouver: What You Get, What It Covers, and Why It Matters

Published July 9, 2026 · Stonnex Roofing

Most homeowners assume a free roof inspection is just a sales visit with a clipboard. Someone walks around for ten minutes, and you end up with a quote you didn’t ask for. That’s not what a proper inspection looks like — and the difference is worth understanding.

A free roof inspection from Stonnex produces a written report with photographs, covers every accessible element of your roof system, and is accepted by major BC insurers and lenders. No obligation, no pressure, no follow-up call you have to dodge.

Here’s exactly what the inspection covers, why the documentation matters, and when it makes sense to book one.

What the Inspection Actually Includes

Written Report with Photographs

The report is the whole point. You get a documented assessment of your roof’s condition — in writing, with photographs — that you can keep, share with your insurer, or hand over during a home sale.

This isn’t a verbal summary at the end of a visit. It’s a formal document. That distinction matters when you’re dealing with an insurance renewal, a sale, or a strata depreciation report.

Visual Inspection of All Accessible Roof Elements

The inspection covers the full roof system, not just the shingles. That includes shingle or membrane condition (wear, cracking, granule loss, lifting), flashing at chimneys, skylights, vents, and wall intersections, ridge caps and hip details, eavestroughs and drainage performance, fascia and soffit condition, and any visible signs of moisture intrusion or deck damage.

In our wet coastal climate — the Lower Mainland receives over 1,200mm of rainfall annually — the weak points are almost always at the transitions: where the roof meets a wall, where a valley channels water, where a vent penetrates the membrane. A thorough inspection pays close attention to those areas.

Optional Drone Imagery

For steep pitches, complex geometry, or areas that aren’t safely accessible from a ladder, drone imagery is available. This isn’t a gimmick. It gives the inspector a clear view of ridge lines, upper valleys, and chimney flashings that are genuinely difficult to assess from ground level or the eaves.

The drone images become part of your report — so your insurer or lender gets visual documentation of hard-to-reach areas, not just a written description.

Optional Thermal Assessment

Thermal imaging identifies moisture trapped beneath a roof membrane or within the insulation layer before it causes visible interior damage. Water that has infiltrated but hasn’t surfaced yet shows up as a temperature differential in the scan.

This is particularly useful for flat roofs and torch-on membrane systems, which are common on coach houses, secondary suites, and modern West Coast architecture across Greater Vancouver. If you suspect a slow leak but can’t find the source, thermal assessment gives you a factual answer.

Why the Report Is Accepted by Insurers and Lenders

BC insurers and mortgage lenders increasingly require documented roof assessments before renewing policies on older homes or approving financing. A verbal opinion from a contractor doesn’t satisfy that requirement. A written report with photographs does.

Stonnex’s inspection reports are accepted by major BC insurers and lenders because they meet the documentation standard those institutions require: written condition assessment, photographic evidence, and a clear summary of the roof’s current state and estimated remaining life.

If you’re approaching an insurance renewal and your provider has flagged your roof’s age, booking an inspection before they ask for one puts you in a stronger position. You arrive with documentation rather than waiting for a demand.

The same logic applies to home sales. A buyer’s inspector will assess your roof regardless. Having your own written report — with drone imagery if applicable — removes uncertainty from the transaction and gives you a factual basis for any negotiation.

This Is Not a Sales Visit

Worth saying plainly: the inspection is not a mechanism to pressure you into a replacement. You receive the report regardless of what you decide to do next.

If your roof has five to eight years of life remaining and needs only minor maintenance, the report will say that. If it’s showing signs you need a new roof and replacement is the honest recommendation, the report will say that too. Either way, you leave with documented information rather than a verbal opinion from someone with a financial interest in the outcome.

A named project manager is assigned to every Stonnex project, but the inspection itself carries no obligation to proceed. The report is yours.

When a Free Roof Inspection Makes Sense

You don’t need to wait for an active leak. These are the situations where booking one is genuinely useful.

Your roof is 15 or more years old. Architectural asphalt shingles in the Lower Mainland typically last 20 to 30 years, but coastal damp, moss accumulation, and temperature cycling accelerate wear. A written assessment at the 15-year mark gives you a useful baseline.

You’re approaching an insurance renewal. Some BC insurers now require documentation of roof condition before renewing policies on homes with roofs over 20 years old. Getting ahead of that request is easier than scrambling to respond under deadline.

You’re preparing to sell. A documented inspection report reduces the negotiating leverage a buyer’s inspector might otherwise have. If you already know the roof’s condition and can demonstrate it in writing, you control the conversation.

You’ve noticed something but aren’t sure if it’s serious. Granule buildup in your gutters, a small interior stain, or shingles that look uneven from the street are all worth a professional look. The only way to know for sure whether it’s cosmetic or structural is a proper assessment.

You’re buying a home. A pre-purchase inspection gives you independent documentation before you close. If the roof needs work in the next few years, you want to know that before the sale completes — not after. What Happens After the Inspection

You receive your written report with photographs. If drone or thermal assessment was included, those findings are documented in the same report.

From there, the decision is entirely yours. If the report shows the roof is in good condition, you have documentation that supports it. If it recommends repair or replacement, you can use the report to get accurate quotes — including Stonnex’s instant online quote tool, which generates real pricing from IKO, GAF, and Malarkey data using your address in 60 seconds, no phone call required.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a free roof inspection in Vancouver actually include?

The inspection covers all accessible roof elements: shingle or membrane condition, flashing, ridge caps, eavestroughs, fascia, soffit, and any visible signs of moisture damage. You receive a written report with photographs at no cost or obligation. Drone imagery and thermal assessment are also available as optional add-ons.

Is the inspection report accepted by BC insurers and lenders?

Yes. Stonnex’s reports are accepted by major BC insurers and lenders. The written format with photographic documentation meets the standard these institutions require for policy renewals, financing approvals, and home sale transactions.

What is thermal assessment and when is it useful?

Thermal imaging detects moisture trapped beneath a roof membrane or within insulation before it causes visible interior damage. It’s most useful for flat roofs, torch-on membrane systems, and situations where a slow or hidden leak is suspected but the source isn’t visible.

Does the free inspection mean I’m committing to a replacement?

No. The inspection carries no obligation. You receive the written report regardless of what you decide afterward. If the roof is in good condition, the report documents that. If repair or replacement is recommended, the decision remains entirely yours.

How long does a roof inspection take?

The on-site portion typically takes 30 to 60 minutes depending on the size and complexity of the roof. Drone imagery adds some time for the flight and review. The written report is delivered after the visit.

Can I use the inspection report when selling my home?

Yes — and it’s a practical advantage. A documented report with photographs gives you factual information about the roof’s condition before a buyer’s inspector produces their own assessment. It reduces uncertainty and gives you a clear basis for any price negotiation related to the roof.

Which areas does Stonnex cover?

Stonnex conducts free roof inspections across the Lower Mainland and Fraser Valley, including West Vancouver, North Vancouver, Vancouver, Burnaby, Richmond, Surrey, Delta, Coquitlam, Langley, Maple Ridge, Abbotsford, and Chilliwack.
