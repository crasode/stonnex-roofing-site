Emergency Roof Repair in Vancouver: What to Do When a Leak Can't Wait

Published August 26, 2026 · Stonnex Roofing

A leak that starts as a faint water stain at 10 pm on a Tuesday does not stay faint. On the coast, rain can fall steadily for days without a break — and a small entry point can saturate insulation, soak drywall, and reach electrical wiring faster than most homeowners expect. What you do in the first hour matters as much as who you call.

This article walks you through the immediate steps, what actually qualifies as an emergency, how to protect your home while you wait for a roofer, and what to expect from the repair process across Vancouver and the broader Lower Mainland.

What counts as a roofing emergency

Not every leak demands a same-day call. But some do. The distinction matters because emergency response costs more, and you want to spend that money when it genuinely protects your home.

Call for emergency repair if you notice any of the following:

  • Active water dripping or pooling inside the home
  • A visible hole, displaced shingles, or torn membrane after a storm
  • A sagging or bulging ceiling — a sign of trapped water above
  • Water near an electrical panel, light fixture, or wiring
  • A sudden surge in water intrusion during heavy rain

If you have a faint stain that's dry to the touch and the rain has stopped, you likely have time to book a standard inspection rather than pay emergency rates. Don't wait weeks, though. What looks minor on the surface often points to something larger underneath.

The first 30 minutes: what to do before anyone arrives

Your goal in the first half hour is to stop water from spreading — not to fix the roof. Leave the roof to a professional.

Inside the home

  1. Place buckets or towels under any active drip points.
  2. If the ceiling is bulging, carefully puncture the lowest point with a screwdriver. This lets water drain in a controlled stream rather than collapsing the drywall suddenly.
  3. Move furniture, electronics, and valuables out of the affected area.
  4. Photograph and video every wet surface, stain, and drip point. Your insurer will want this.
  5. If water is near any electrical fixture or panel, turn off the circuit breaker for that area and leave it off until a professional clears it.

Temporary measures on the roof (only if safe)

If you can safely access a flat or low-pitch roof and you have heavy-duty polyethylene sheeting on hand, covering the affected area with a weighted tarp can slow water entry. Do this only in dry conditions, with proper footwear, and never on a wet pitched roof. A fall is a far worse outcome than a wet ceiling.

Honestly, most Vancouver homeowners are better off staying off the roof entirely and calling a roofer straight away.

Calling a roofer: what to say and what to ask

When you call, be specific. Tell the roofer:

  • Your address and the approximate age of the roof
  • Where water is entering inside — ceiling, wall, around a skylight, near a chimney
  • Whether you can see any visible damage from the ground
  • How long the leak has been active
  • Whether it's still raining

Ask directly whether they carry WorkSafeBC registration and liability insurance. Any legitimate contractor serving Greater Vancouver will confirm both without hesitation. If they hesitate, that's your answer.

Also ask whether the repair comes with a written report. That documentation protects you when you file an insurance claim and when you eventually sell the home.

Common emergency repair scenarios in Vancouver

Storm damage to asphalt shingles

Wind-driven rain and the occasional windstorm across Metro Vancouver can lift or crack architectural shingles — particularly on roofs past the 15-year mark. A roofer will typically re-secure or replace the damaged shingles, check the underlayment beneath, and seal any exposed decking.

If the roof is showing widespread wear alongside the storm damage, the repair conversation may quickly become a replacement conversation. The signs you need a new roof will help you understand where that threshold sits.

Flat roof membrane failure

Flat and torch-on membrane roofs are common on Vancouver coach houses, laneway homes, and modern infill builds. When a membrane fails, water pools instead of draining and finds any seam or blister to push through. Emergency repairs typically involve cleaning the affected section, cutting out the damaged membrane, and torching a new section in place. This is not a DIY repair.

Skylight and flashing leaks

A significant share of emergency calls in Vancouver trace back to failed flashing around skylights, chimneys, or roof penetrations — not the field of the roof itself. The flashing seal degrades over time, and once it goes, water tracks down interior walls and framing rather than dripping straight down. That makes the source harder to find and the damage easier to miss.

Cedar shake splitting or displacement

Cedar shake roofs on older homes in North Vancouver, West Vancouver, and parts of Burnaby and Vancouver proper are prone to splitting as they age — especially after freeze-thaw cycles or sustained wet weather. Individual shakes can be replaced during an emergency call, but widespread splitting usually means the roof is past its service life.

What a proper emergency repair includes

A rushed patch that stops the leak today but leaves the underlying cause untouched will bring you back to the same situation within months. A proper emergency repair should cover:

  • Identification of the actual entry point, not just the visible wet spot inside
  • Temporary or permanent repair of that entry point
  • A written summary of what was found and what was done
  • Clear guidance on whether a full inspection or replacement is needed

At Stonnex, every inspection produces a written report accepted by major BC insurers and lenders. If your emergency repair leads to a claim, having that documentation from the start makes the process significantly smoother.

After the emergency: what comes next

Once the immediate leak is under control, book a full roof inspection within a few weeks. Emergency repairs address the acute problem — they don't give you the full picture of your roof's condition.

A thorough inspection will tell you:

  • Whether the rest of the roof is structurally sound
  • How much service life remains
  • Whether hidden rot or moisture damage developed before the leak was caught
  • What a full repair or replacement would cost

Understanding roof replacement cost in Metro Vancouver before that conversation happens puts you in a better position to evaluate any quote you receive.

Insurance and documentation

Most standard BC homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental water damage from a roof failure. They do not cover damage from gradual deterioration or deferred maintenance. That distinction matters when you file a claim.

Document everything: photos before any repairs, photos during, the written report from your roofer, and all receipts. If your insurer sends an adjuster, a professional written report from a WorkSafeBC-registered contractor carries real weight.

If your roof is approaching the end of its expected life, your insurer may also ask for a condition report at renewal. Inspection reports from Stonnex are accepted by major BC insurers and lenders, which simplifies that process considerably.

Choosing the right roofing material after an emergency

If your emergency repair reveals the roof needs full replacement, the material decision is worth making carefully rather than defaulting to whatever was there before. In our wet coastal climate, the demands on roofing materials are different from drier inland regions — and that affects which options actually hold up.

The best roofing material for the BC coast covers the trade-offs between architectural asphalt, standing-seam metal, cedar shake, and membrane systems in detail. The short version: standing-seam metal performs exceptionally well in sustained wet conditions, while architectural asphalt offers the best value-to-lifespan ratio for most residential budgets.

A note on storm chasers and pressure tactics

After a major storm in Metro Vancouver, contractors from outside the region sometimes canvas neighbourhoods offering fast, cheap repairs. Some are legitimate. Many are not.

Watch for these red flags:

  • No WorkSafeBC registration number provided on request
  • Cash-only payment required upfront
  • No written contract or scope of work
  • Pressure to sign before they “move to the next neighbourhood”
  • No local address or verifiable business presence

A legitimate roofer serving Vancouver will give you time to review a written quote, confirm their credentials, and ask questions. No-pressure isn't a bonus — it's the baseline.

Frequently asked questions

What qualifies as a roofing emergency in Vancouver?

Active water entering the home, visible structural damage after a storm, a sagging ceiling, or water near electrical systems all qualify. A dry stain that isn't actively growing can typically wait for a scheduled inspection rather than an emergency call.

Should I get on the roof myself to stop the leak?

Only if you can do so safely, in dry conditions, on a flat or very low-pitch roof, and only to place a tarp. Never access a wet pitched roof. The fall risk is serious, and most homeowners are better off staying inside and calling a roofer immediately.

Will my home insurance cover emergency roof repairs?

Most standard BC homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental damage, but not gradual deterioration. Document everything with photos and get a written report from your roofer before filing a claim. Reports from WorkSafeBC-registered contractors carry the most weight with adjusters.

How do I know if an emergency repair is enough or if I need a full replacement?

Your roofer should tell you clearly after the repair. If the rest of the roof is past its service life or showing widespread wear, a patch won't hold long. A full inspection with a written report gives you the information you need to make that call without pressure.

How long does an emergency roof repair take in Vancouver?

Most emergency repairs involving shingle replacement, flashing, or membrane patching take a few hours on-site. The timeline depends on the scope of damage, weather conditions, and material availability. Your roofer should give you a realistic estimate before starting work.

What should I ask a roofer before hiring them for emergency work?

Confirm WorkSafeBC registration, liability insurance, and whether the repair includes a written report. Ask for a clear scope of work in writing before anything starts. A legitimate contractor will provide all of this without hesitation.

Can emergency repairs be done in the rain?

Some repairs — applying a temporary tarp or sealing small penetrations with roofing cement — can be done in light rain. Permanent repairs involving torch-on membrane or shingle installation require dry conditions for proper adhesion and safety. A roofer may apply a temporary fix in wet weather and return for the permanent repair once conditions allow.
