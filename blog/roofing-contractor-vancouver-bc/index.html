Roofing Contractor Vancouver BC: What a Licensed Local Roofer Should Offer

Published July 20, 2026 · Stonnex Roofing

Choosing a roofing contractor in Greater Vancouver or the Fraser Valley is one of the more consequential decisions you'll make as a homeowner. The Lower Mainland receives over 1,200mm of rainfall annually, and that kind of sustained moisture exposes every weakness in a roof. Going with the lowest quote or the first name that comes up in a search can leave you with a failed installation, a voided warranty, and no one to call.

Here's what a licensed roofing contractor in Vancouver BC should actually offer — and what separates a contractor worth hiring from one you'll regret.

Verified Credentials, Not Just a Business Card

A legitimate BC roofing contractor carries specific, verifiable credentials. WorkSafeBC registration protects workers on your property and shields you from liability if someone is injured on the job. You can confirm status directly through the provincial registry.

BBB Accreditation means the contractor has agreed to binding dispute resolution and meets standards for transparency and responsiveness. Liability coverage of at least $5 million is the floor for any serious residential or commercial project in Greater Vancouver.

Specialty certifications matter too. A VELUX-certified installer, for example, has completed manufacturer-specific training for skylight systems — a distinction that affects both installation quality and whether your product warranty holds.

Pricing You Can See Before the Sales Visit

One of the most common frustrations homeowners have with roofing contractors is the runaround before getting a number. You fill out a form, wait two days, sit through a sales visit, and still leave without a clear figure.

A contractor worth hiring gives you real pricing upfront. Stonnex uses an address-based quote tool that pulls live data from IKO, GAF, and Malarkey and returns a real price estimate in approximately 60 seconds. No phone call required. No appointment needed just to find out whether a project fits your budget.

Pricing transparency is a signal of contractor confidence. A roofer who knows their costs and stands behind their materials doesn't need to obscure numbers until they're sitting across from you.

A Named Project Manager on Every Job

Most roofing companies assign a crew and move on. You meet a salesperson, sign a contract, and then deal with whoever shows up. When a question comes up mid-project, there's no clear person to call.

A licensed contractor should assign a named project manager to every job — someone who is your single point of contact from inspection through completion. They know your roof, your timeline, and your material selections. This isn't a perk reserved for large commercial projects. It's the standard you should expect on any residential replacement or repair, whether you're in Burnaby, Surrey, Langley, or anywhere else in the region.

Written Inspection Reports, Not Verbal Assessments

When a contractor inspects your roof, the output should be a written report with photographs. Verbal assessments aren't accepted by BC insurers or lenders. If you're preparing to sell, refinancing, or filing an insurance claim, you need documentation that holds up.

A proper inspection report covers current condition, areas of concern, estimated remaining service life, and recommended next steps. Drone imagery and thermal assessment are available as optional additions for complex or aging roofs. The inspection itself should cost you nothing and carry no obligation.

If you're unsure whether your roof has reached the point where a replacement conversation makes sense, reviewing the signs you need a new roof is a useful starting point before booking anything.

Material Knowledge Matched to BC's Climate

Vancouver's coastal climate demands specific material knowledge. Salt air, sustained moisture, and freeze-thaw cycles at higher elevations in Maple Ridge or Abbotsford create different stresses than a dry interior climate. A contractor who sells the same product everywhere without adapting to local conditions isn't doing their job.

The right contractor works across multiple roof profiles: architectural asphalt shingles, cedar shake, standing seam metal, and slate. For flat roofs on modern West Coast homes, coach houses, or secondary suites, torch-on membrane is the appropriate solution. Each material performs differently under BC's rainfall volumes, and your contractor should be able to explain those trade-offs clearly.

If you're weighing your options, a detailed look at roofing materials suited to the BC coast can help you understand what holds up best in this specific climate before you commit.

Warranties That Actually Hold

A manufacturer warranty is only as good as the installation behind it. Many contractors mention warranties in their marketing without explaining the conditions that can void them — including some that require annual maintenance fees just to keep coverage active.

A licensed contractor should be able to offer manufacturer warranties up to 50 years on qualifying products, with no hidden annual fee required to maintain validity. The warranty should cover both materials and workmanship, and the contractor should walk you through exactly what voids it before you sign anything.

When you're thinking through the full cost of a roof replacement in Metro Vancouver, warranty depth is part of the calculation. A lower upfront price paired with a weaker warranty often costs more over the life of the roof.

Strata and Commercial Capacity

If you manage a strata or multi-family building in Greater Vancouver, your requirements go beyond what a residential-only contractor can handle. You need insurance-ready assessments, documentation formatted for depreciation reports, phased scheduling that minimizes disruption to residents, and formal contractor documentation for council approval.

A contractor serving this segment should have direct experience with strata processes — not just residential work scaled up. The documentation requirements and scheduling complexity are genuinely different, and that experience shows in the work.

Questions to Ask Before You Hire

Before signing with any roofing contractor in Vancouver BC, ask these directly:

  • Can you provide your WorkSafeBC registration number?
  • What is your liability coverage amount?
  • Who is the named project manager on my job?
  • What manufacturer warranties are available on the materials you're recommending?
  • Will I receive a written inspection report with photographs?
  • How is your pricing generated, and can I see it before we meet?

A contractor who answers these questions clearly and specifically is worth your time. One who deflects or stays vague is telling you something important.

FAQs

What credentials should a roofing contractor in Vancouver BC have?
At minimum: WorkSafeBC registration, BBB Accreditation, and liability insurance of at least $5 million. Specialty certifications — such as VELUX installer status or manufacturer-specific training — add further credibility and directly affect warranty validity.

How do I get a roofing quote in Vancouver without a sales visit?
Stonnex offers an address-based quote tool that generates real pricing from IKO, GAF, and Malarkey data in approximately 60 seconds. Enter your address and the tool returns a price estimate — no phone call, no appointment required.

What should a roof inspection report include?
A proper written report covers current roof condition, areas of concern, estimated remaining service life, and recommended next steps. It should include photographs and be formatted for acceptance by BC insurers and lenders. Drone imagery and thermal assessment are available as optional additions.

Why does it matter who my project manager is?
A named project manager is your single point of contact from start to finish. Without one, questions and issues during a job have no clear owner. Accountability needs to be built into the project structure — not left to chance.

What roofing materials work best in Greater Vancouver's climate?
Architectural asphalt shingles are the most common choice for cost and durability. Cedar shake performs well aesthetically but requires more maintenance given BC's moisture levels. Standing seam metal is highly durable and handles heavy rainfall well. Torch-on membrane is the standard for flat roofs. The right choice depends on your roof profile, budget, and how long you plan to stay in the home.

How long do roofing warranties last in BC?
Manufacturer warranties on qualifying products can extend up to 50 years. The length depends on the product tier and installation quality. Any warranty that requires annual fees to stay valid is worth scrutinizing before you commit.

Do roofing contractors in Vancouver serve strata buildings?
Yes, but not all have the documentation experience that strata work requires. Look for a contractor who can provide insurance-ready assessments, depreciation report support, phased scheduling, and formal council documentation.

The standard you should hold any roofing contractor in Vancouver BC to isn't complicated: verified credentials, transparent pricing, a named person accountable for your job, written documentation, and warranties explained clearly before you sign. Those aren't extras — they're the baseline.
