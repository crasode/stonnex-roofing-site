Published July 7, 2026 · Stonnex Roofing

Two questions come up before anything else when Langley homeowners start thinking about their roof: what's this going to cost, and how do I know I'm hiring someone I can trust? Which Roofing Material Makes Sense for Langley

Langley gets over 1,200mm of annual rainfall and sits close enough to the coast that moisture management matters year-round. The material you choose affects not just the up-front cost but how often you'll be dealing with moss, granule loss, and re-caulking over the next two decades.

Architectural Asphalt Shingles

This is the most common choice across Langley's suburban neighbourhoods. Modern architectural shingles from IKO, GAF, or Malarkey carry manufacturer warranties up to 50 years and hold up well in wet coastal conditions when installed with proper underlayment and ventilation. They're also the most cost-accessible option on the market.

Best for: Homeowners replacing an aging asphalt roof who want reliable performance and strong warranty coverage without the premium cost of metal or slate.

Cedar Shake

Cedar is common on older Langley homes and acreage properties, and it looks exceptional when it's well maintained. The honest trade-off is that cedar demands more upkeep in a wet climate — moss management is not optional if you want the shake to last. When cedar is near the end of its life, replacement costs run higher than asphalt due to both material cost and installation complexity.

Best for: Homeowners replacing an existing cedar roof who want to preserve the character of an older property and are prepared for the maintenance commitment.

Standing Seam Metal

Metal is the longest-lasting option and sheds moss and water beautifully — a real advantage in Langley's climate. The trade-off is higher up-front cost. Over a 40- to 50-year horizon, the total cost of ownership often compares favourably to two asphalt replacements.

Best for: Homeowners planning to stay long-term, building new, or replacing a roof on a high-value property where longevity and low maintenance justify the initial outlay.

Flat and Torch-On Membrane Systems

Langley has a growing number of coach houses, secondary suites, and modern flat-roof additions. Torch-on membrane systems — SBS membrane, or styrene-butadiene-styrene — are the standard for these applications on the West Coast. Properly installed, they handle standing water and freeze-thaw cycles without the cracking issues that plagued older flat roof systems.

Best for: Coach houses, garages, additions, and any structure with a low-slope or flat roof profile.

What to Verify Before Hiring a Langley Roofer

The Fraser Valley has no shortage of roofing contractors, and credential gaps are common. Before signing anything, confirm the following.

WorkSafeBC registration. Any contractor working on your property must be registered. You can verify this directly on the WorkSafeBC website using the contractor's name. If they can't provide their registration number, stop there.

BBB Accreditation. Accreditation requires meeting BBB standards for transparency and complaint resolution. It's not a guarantee of quality, but it's a meaningful baseline check.

Manufacturer certifications. A contractor certified by IKO, GAF, or Malarkey has completed product-specific training. This matters because manufacturer warranties often require certified installation — a gap there can void coverage you're paying for.

Named accountability. Ask who your point of contact will be from start to finish. A contractor who can't name a specific project manager is one where accountability diffuses quickly if something goes wrong.

The Free Inspection: What It Is and Why It Matters

A lot of Langley homeowners aren't sure whether they need a repair or a full replacement. The only way to know for sure is a proper inspection — and that means more than someone walking the perimeter.

A thorough inspection covers decking condition, flashing integrity, ventilation, and the condition of penetrations around chimneys, skylights, and plumbing stacks. Drone imagery can assess areas that aren't safely accessible on foot, and thermal assessment can identify moisture intrusion that isn't visible on the surface.

FAQs: Roofing Contractor Langley BC

How much does a roof replacement cost in Langley in 2026?

For a standard suburban home with architectural asphalt shingles, expect roughly $12,000–$20,000 installed. Cedar shake runs $18,000–$30,000 or more, and standing seam metal typically falls between $25,000 and $45,000. Flat membrane systems for coach houses and secondary suites range from $6,000 to $14,000. Exact pricing depends on roof size, pitch, decking condition, and material tier.

How do I verify a Langley roofing contractor is legitimate?

Confirm WorkSafeBC registration using the contractor's number on the WorkSafeBC website. Check BBB Accreditation status. Ask about manufacturer certifications for the materials being used, and confirm who your named project manager will be throughout the job.

What's the difference between a repair and a full replacement?

A repair addresses localized damage — a few failed shingles, a cracked flashing, a single leak point. A replacement makes sense when the roof is past 20–25 years, granule loss is widespread, or multiple failure points exist. A written inspection report is the most reliable way to determine which applies to your roof.

Is a free roof inspection actually free, or is it a sales pitch?

A proper free inspection produces a written report with photographs and no obligation to proceed. Stonnex's inspection reports are accepted by major BC insurers and lenders. If a contractor's “free inspection” doesn't result in written documentation, it's a quote visit — not an inspection.

What roofing materials work best on Langley acreage properties?

Standing seam metal performs exceptionally well on larger rural properties due to its longevity, low maintenance, and moss resistance. Cedar shake is common on older acreage homes and suits the aesthetic, though it requires consistent moss management in the Fraser Valley's wet climate. Architectural asphalt shingles are a strong mid-range option for any property type.

Do Langley roofers work on coach houses and secondary suites?

Yes. Flat and torch-on membrane systems are the standard for low-slope and flat roofs on coach houses, garages, and secondary suites. Not all contractors carry the equipment and experience for membrane work alongside conventional pitched roofing, so confirm this before hiring.

How long does a roof replacement take in Langley?

Most residential replacements take one to three days depending on size, pitch, and complexity. Larger acreage properties or jobs with significant decking repair can run longer. Your project manager should give you a clear schedule before work begins.
