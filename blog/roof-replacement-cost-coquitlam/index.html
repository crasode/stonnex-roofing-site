Roof Replacement Cost in Coquitlam and Port Coquitlam in 2026

Published July 12, 2026 · Stonnex Roofing

If you own a home in Coquitlam, Port Coquitlam, or anywhere in the Tri-Cities and you're trying to figure out what a roof replacement will actually cost, the honest answer is: it depends on four or five things specific to your house. But that doesn't mean you have to wait for a contractor to call you back before you get a number.

This guide covers realistic 2026 price ranges by material, what drives costs in the Tri-Cities specifically, and how to get a real quote tied to your address in about 60 seconds.

What Roof Replacements Cost in Coquitlam and Port Coquitlam in 2026

Most detached homes in Coquitlam and Port Coquitlam sit between 1,800 and 2,800 square feet of living space — which typically translates to a roof area of roughly 20 to 32 squares (one square equals 100 square feet of roof surface). That size range, combined with your material choice, sets the floor and ceiling of your project cost.

What Moves the Price Up or Down in the Tri-Cities

Roof pitch and complexity

Coquitlam and Port Coquitlam have a wide mix of housing stock — flat-roofed coach houses and townhomes near Lincoln Station, steeply pitched heritage-style homes in Burke Mountain and Mary Hill. A steep pitch (above 7:12) takes more time, more safety equipment, and generates more material waste. Multiple valleys, dormers, or skylights add cost for the same reasons.

Decking condition

Tear-off is when surprises show up. In our wet coastal climate, homes with even minor leak history often have soft spots or rot in the plywood decking underneath. Replacing damaged decking runs roughly $80 to $120 per sheet — and it's not optional if the rot is there. A good contractor will photograph what they find and price the repair separately, not fold it into a lump sum.

Material grade and manufacturer

Not all architectural shingles are equal. An IKO Cambridge and a Malarkey Vista AR are both architectural asphalt, but they carry different warranties, different algae-resistance ratings, and different price points. The manufacturer matters, and so does the warranty tier. Asphalt products from IKO, GAF, and Malarkey can extend up to 50 years depending on the product line and installation standard.

Labour and timing

Roofing labour in the Lower Mainland has tightened over the past few years. Experienced crews book further out, and that affects scheduling more than price. Spring and early summer are the busiest windows. If you're planning ahead rather than responding to an emergency, late summer or fall typically gives you more flexibility.

Architectural Asphalt: The Most Common Choice in Coquitlam

Architectural asphalt shingles — also called laminate shingles — are the most installed roof type across Coquitlam and Port Coquitlam. They handle the region's 1,200mm-plus of annual rainfall, resist moss better than older three-tab shingles, and come in profiles that suit both modern and traditional homes.

Best for: Homeowners who want a reliable, well-warranted roof at a mid-range price — especially on standard-pitch homes in established neighbourhoods like Westwood Plateau, Ranch Park, or Riverwood.

The trade-off is lifespan. At 20 to 30 years, asphalt won't outlast metal or slate. If you're planning to stay in your home for decades, it's worth comparing the long-term cost per year across materials before you decide.

Cedar Shake: Still Common, but Maintenance-Dependent

Cedar shake is still found on many older Coquitlam and Port Coquitlam homes — particularly those built in the 1980s and 1990s. It looks excellent and performs well when maintained. In a wet coastal climate, though, moss and moisture management are not optional. Without regular cleaning and treatment, cedar shake can fail well before its expected lifespan.

Best for: Homeowners replacing an existing cedar roof who want to preserve the look of an older home and are committed to a maintenance schedule.

If you're weighing whether to stay with shake or switch materials entirely, the guide to the best roofing materials for the BC coast covers that comparison in detail.

Metal Roofing: Higher Cost, Longer Horizon

Standing seam metal is growing in popularity across newer Coquitlam developments — particularly in Burke Mountain and the newer Port Coquitlam subdivisions. It sheds moss and water naturally, handles snow load well, and in most cases will outlast the mortgage. The up-front cost is significantly higher than asphalt, but the cost per year of service often works out favourably over a 40-plus-year lifespan. A real advantage here for anyone planning to stay long-term.

Best for: Homeowners who want to replace the roof once and not think about it again — new builds, long-term owners, or anyone prioritising durability over up-front cost.

Flat and Torch-On Membrane Systems

A portion of Coquitlam and Port Coquitlam housing stock includes coach houses, secondary suites, and additions with flat or low-slope roofs. These need a different system entirely. Torch-on membrane — a modified bitumen product using SBS (styrene-butadiene-styrene) technology — is the standard for flat roofing in BC. Applied in layers with a torch, it creates a durable, waterproof surface well suited to the coastal climate.

Flat roof replacement costs less per square than pitched roofing, but the system depends on proper drainage design to perform. Ponding water is the main failure mode, and it's entirely preventable with correct installation.

Signs You Might Be Closer to Replacement Than Repair

Not sure whether you need a full replacement or just a repair? A few things point toward replacement: shingles that are curling, cracking, or losing granules at scale; a roof past the 20-year mark; interior staining that suggests ongoing rather than isolated leakage; or moss coverage that has worked its way into the shingle surface rather than sitting on top.

FAQs: Roof Replacement in Coquitlam and Port Coquitlam

How much does a roof replacement cost in Coquitlam in 2026?

For a typical detached home with an architectural asphalt shingle roof, expect to pay between $10,000 and $18,000 installed. Cedar shake runs $18,000 to $32,000, and standing seam metal starts around $28,000. The exact cost depends on your roof's size, pitch, complexity, and material.

How long does a roof replacement take in the Tri-Cities?

Most standard residential replacements take one to three days. Larger or more complex roofs — or jobs requiring significant decking repair — can run longer. Weather is a factor, particularly in the shoulder seasons.

Do I need a permit for a roof replacement in Coquitlam?

Both Coquitlam and Port Coquitlam generally require a building permit for full roof replacements. Your contractor should handle the permit application as part of the project. Confirm this before signing any contract.

What roofing material is best for the Tri-Cities climate?

Architectural asphalt shingles are the most practical choice for most homeowners — a solid balance of cost, lifespan, and performance in a wet coastal climate. Metal roofing offers the best long-term durability. Cedar shake performs well but needs ongoing moss management to hold up here.

Can I get a roof replacement quote without a contractor visiting my home?

Yes. Stonnex's instant online quote tool generates real pricing from IKO, GAF, and Malarkey data using your home's address — a price range in about 60 seconds, no phone call required. A free in-person inspection with a written report is also available if you want something more detailed.

What warranties are available on a new roof in Coquitlam?

Manufacturer warranties on asphalt shingles from IKO, GAF, and Malarkey can extend up to 50 years depending on the product tier and installation standard. Workmanship warranties vary by contractor. Ask for both in writing before any work starts.

How do I know if I need a repair or a full replacement?

Age is the most reliable indicator. If your roof is past 20 years and showing widespread granule loss, curling, or cracking, replacement is usually more cost-effective than ongoing repairs. A free inspection with a written report is the most reliable way to get a clear answer specific to your roof.
