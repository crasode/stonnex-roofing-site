Roofing Companies in North Vancouver: Questions Every Homeowner Should Ask

Published July 29, 2026 · Stonnex Roofing

Hiring a roofing contractor in North Vancouver is one of the more significant decisions you'll make as a homeowner. The North Shore's wet winters, heavy rainfall, and steep-pitched homes mean a poor choice doesn't just cost money — it costs you years of water damage, voided warranties, and contractor disputes. Asking the right questions before signing anything is what separates a smooth project from a stressful one.

Here's exactly what to ask any roofing company in North Vancouver, why each question matters, and what a strong answer actually looks like.

Why North Vancouver Roofs Face Specific Pressures

North Vancouver sits at the base of the Coast Mountains, which means it receives significantly more precipitation than most of the Lower Mainland. Homes in Lynn Valley, Deep Cove, and Upper Lonsdale regularly deal with moss accumulation, ice damming near eaves, and accelerated shingle wear from sustained moisture exposure.

Cedar shake roofs — common on older North Shore homes — are particularly vulnerable to moisture retention and splitting when maintenance is deferred. Architectural asphalt shingles and standing seam metal are increasingly popular replacements because both handle BC's coastal climate more predictably over a 20- to 30-year horizon.

If you're noticing granule loss in your gutters, curling shingle edges, or dark streaking on your roof deck, those are concrete signs you may need a new roof rather than another repair cycle.

The Questions That Actually Matter

1. Are You WorkSafeBC Registered and Carrying Liability Insurance?

Start here, not at the end. Any legitimate roofing contractor operating in BC must be registered with WorkSafeBC. If a worker is injured on your property and the contractor isn't registered, the liability can fall on you. Ask for the WorkSafeBC registration number and verify it online — it takes two minutes.

Liability coverage matters just as much. You want to see at least $2 million; $5 million is standard for contractors working on higher-value homes. Ask for a certificate of insurance naming your address, not just a verbal confirmation.

2. Who Will Be My Point of Contact Throughout the Project?

This question reveals more about how a contractor actually operates than almost anything else you could ask. Many roofing companies assign a salesperson for the quote, then hand the job to a crew you've never met. When something goes wrong mid-project, you're left calling a general number and hoping someone calls back.

A contractor who assigns a named project manager to every job gives you a specific person accountable from inspection through final walkthrough. On a $15,000 to $30,000 project, that accountability structure isn't a nice-to-have — it's essential.

3. What Warranties Are You Offering, and Who Backs Them?

There are two distinct warranties on any roofing job: the manufacturer's material warranty and the contractor's workmanship warranty. They are not the same thing.

Material warranties from manufacturers like IKO, GAF, and Malarkey can extend up to 50 years depending on the product tier and installation certification. Workmanship warranties vary widely — some contractors offer two years, others ten. Get both in writing before you commit. Also ask whether the manufacturer warranty has conditions attached to keep it valid, such as registered installation records or annual fees.

4. Can You Provide a Written Inspection Report Before Quoting?

A reputable contractor inspects your roof before pricing the job, not after. That inspection should produce a written report with photographs documenting current conditions, problem areas, and recommended scope of work. This protects you in two ways: it gives you a clear record of what was found, and it ensures the quote reflects actual conditions rather than a guess from the driveway.

If you're dealing with an insurance claim or preparing for a home sale, that written report needs to be in a format accepted by BC insurers and lenders. Ask specifically whether it meets that standard before booking.

5. What Roofing Materials Do You Work With, and Are You Certified to Install Them?

Not every contractor is certified to install every product. VELUX skylight installation requires specific certification. Many manufacturers only extend their full warranty when the product is installed by a certified contractor. Ask which manufacturers the company is certified with, and ask to see documentation if warranty coverage is on the line.

For North Vancouver homes specifically, ask whether the contractor has hands-on experience with steep-pitch installations and cedar shake replacement. These are not interchangeable skill sets with standard asphalt shingle work on a flat suburban roof.

6. How Is Your Pricing Structured, and Can I See It Before Committing?

Pricing opacity is one of the most consistent complaints homeowners have about roofing contractors. You call for a quote, someone comes out, and you're handed a number with minimal breakdown and no easy way to evaluate whether it's reasonable.

Some contractors now offer address-based instant quote tools that pull live pricing data from manufacturer sources and return a real number in about 60 seconds — no phone call required. That kind of transparency is worth something. It lets you enter the conversation with a baseline rather than starting from zero.

For a broader look at what full replacements actually cost across the region, the roof replacement cost guide for Metro Vancouver breaks down pricing by material type and home size.

7. What Happens If Something Goes Wrong After the Job Is Done?

Ask this directly. A contractor who responds with "we stand behind our work" is telling you something. A contractor who describes a specific process, a named contact, and a documented warranty claim procedure is telling you something very different.

Find out whether the contractor holds BBB Accreditation, which requires meeting defined standards for complaint resolution and business transparency. Check whether there are any unresolved complaints on file. It's publicly available and takes five minutes.

What Strong Answers Look Like in Practice

When you put these questions to any roofing company in North Vancouver, you're stress-testing their operations. Strong answers share a few consistent traits:

  • They're specific, not vague. "WorkSafeBC number 12345678" beats "yes, we're registered."
  • They come with documentation — a certificate of insurance, a written warranty, a named project manager's direct contact.
  • They don't pressure you to decide before you're ready. Any contractor pushing hard for a same-day signature is removing your ability to compare properly.

The goal isn't to catch anyone out. It's to confirm that the contractor you're considering has the credentials, structure, and communication habits to manage a significant project on your home without surprises.

Comparing Multiple Quotes

Once you have two or three quotes in hand, the comparison isn't just about the number at the bottom. Look at what each quote actually includes: tear-off and disposal, underlayment specification, flashing replacement, ventilation assessment, and cleanup. A lower quote that excludes flashing replacement on a 25-year-old roof isn't actually lower — it's deferred cost.

Compare warranty terms side by side as well. A 25-year architectural shingle installed by a certified contractor with a full manufacturer warranty is a different product than the same shingle installed by an uncertified crew. The material is identical; the coverage isn't.

FAQs

What credentials should I verify before hiring a roofing company in North Vancouver?

At minimum, confirm WorkSafeBC registration (verifiable online), liability insurance of at least $2 million, and BBB Accreditation or an equivalent verifiable credential. Ask for a certificate of insurance naming your property address.

How do I know if my North Vancouver roof needs replacement or just repairs?

Granule loss in gutters, curling or cracked shingles, visible daylight through the attic, and active leaks are all indicators that replacement may be more cost-effective than continued repairs. A written inspection report with photographs gives you an objective basis for that decision.

What is a manufacturer warranty versus a workmanship warranty on a roof?

A manufacturer warranty covers defects in the roofing material itself and can extend up to 50 years depending on the product and installation certification. A workmanship warranty covers the contractor's installation quality and is typically shorter. You need both, in writing, before signing a contract.

Why does it matter whether a contractor is certified to install specific products?

Manufacturers like IKO, GAF, and Malarkey often require certified installation to validate their full warranty terms. An uncertified installation may use the same shingle, but the homeowner may not be eligible for full warranty coverage if a claim arises.

Can I get a roofing quote without someone coming to my home first?

Yes. Address-based instant quote tools pull live pricing data from manufacturer sources and return real pricing in about 60 seconds with no phone call required. This gives you a baseline before committing to an in-person visit.

What should a roofing inspection report include for it to be accepted by BC insurers?

The report should include written documentation of current roof conditions, identified problem areas, photographs of findings, and a clear scope of recommended work. Ask specifically whether the format is accepted by major BC insurers and lenders before booking.

How do I compare roofing quotes fairly when the numbers look different?

Look beyond the total price. Compare what each quote includes: tear-off and disposal, underlayment type, flashing replacement, ventilation work, and cleanup. Also compare warranty terms, manufacturer certifications, and whether a named project manager is assigned. A lower number that excludes flashing or uses a lower-tier shingle is not an equivalent comparison.
