Roof Replacement Cost in Burnaby in 2026: What to Budget for Every Roof Type

Published July 6, 2026 · Stonnex Roofing

Burnaby homeowners ask this question more than almost any other: what does a roof replacement actually cost? Honestly, the answer depends on your roof type, your home’s size, and a few Burnaby-specific factors that most generic guides skip entirely. This article gives you real 2026 price ranges for every major roofing material, explains what drives the final number up or down, and helps you figure out where your home sits in that range.

What Roof Replacement Costs in Burnaby in 2026

Here are the working price ranges for a full roof replacement on a typical Burnaby detached home — tear-off, materials, and labour included.

Roof TypeTypical Range (2026)Lifespan
Architectural asphalt shingles$9,000 – $16,00025–30 years
Cedar shake$18,000 – $32,00030–40 years (with maintenance)
Standing seam metal$22,000 – $45,000+50+ years
Flat membrane (torch-on / SBS)$8,000 – $18,00020–30 years

These ranges assume a single-storey or standard two-storey detached home between 1,500 and 2,500 square feet of living space. Larger homes, steeper pitches, and more complex rooflines push costs toward the upper end.

The cheapest roof you can buy today is rarely the cheapest roof over a 20-year period. Material cost and total cost of ownership are two different numbers.

Why Burnaby Homes Have Specific Cost Drivers

Burnaby’s housing stock is genuinely varied. Post-war bungalows in Burnaby Heights, 1970s split-levels in South Burnaby, newer strata townhomes near Metrotown and Brentwood — each type comes with its own cost considerations.

Older homes (pre-1990) often have a layer or two of existing shingles already on the deck. Tearing off multiple layers adds labour time and disposal costs, typically $500 to $1,500 depending on volume. Decking condition is another factor: older plywood or board sheathing sometimes shows rot or soft spots that only appear once the old roof is stripped. Replacing damaged decking runs roughly $80 to $120 per sheet.

Strata townhomes in Burnaby present a different challenge. Scheduling has to work around shared walls and common property access. If your strata council is coordinating a multi-unit replacement, documentation requirements add a layer of planning — something Stonnex handles directly, including insurance-ready assessments.

Burnaby’s wet coastal climate matters too. With over 1,200mm of annual rainfall, moss and algae growth accelerate material wear faster here than in drier parts of the province. Any cost estimate should factor in moss management treatments, especially for cedar shake and architectural asphalt.

Architectural Asphalt Shingles: $9,000 – $16,000

Architectural shingles — also called dimensional or laminate shingles — are the most common choice on Burnaby homes. They’re available from IKO, GAF, and Malarkey in a wide range of profiles, with manufacturer warranties that can extend up to 50 years depending on the product line and installation method.

The cost range is wide because pitch, complexity, and product tier all move the number. A straightforward gable roof on a 1,600-square-foot bungalow sits toward the lower end. A hip-and-valley roof with multiple dormers or skylights sits toward the upper end.

Best for: Burnaby homeowners who want reliable, well-warranted protection at a mid-range price — particularly those planning to stay in the home for 15 to 25 more years.

Cedar Shake: $18,000 – $32,000

Cedar shake remains popular in older Burnaby neighbourhoods for its appearance and natural insulating properties. The trade-off is maintenance. In our wet coastal climate, cedar needs regular moss treatment and inspection to reach its full lifespan. Neglected cedar can start showing serious deterioration at 15 years rather than 30.

Installation is more labour-intensive than asphalt, which accounts for a significant portion of the higher cost. Material costs have also climbed with lumber prices. If your existing cedar is near the end of its life, it’s worth reviewing the signs you need a new roof before deciding whether repair or full replacement makes more sense.

Best for: Burnaby homeowners in established neighbourhoods who value the aesthetic, are committed to ongoing maintenance, and plan to stay in the home long-term.

Standing Seam Metal: $22,000 – $45,000+

Metal is the longest-lasting option and sheds moss and water beautifully — a real advantage in Burnaby’s climate. Standing seam panels interlock without exposed fasteners, which eliminates the most common failure point on older screw-down metal systems.

The upfront cost is the honest trade-off. A standing seam metal roof on a larger Burnaby home can approach or exceed $45,000. Over a 50-year lifespan with minimal maintenance, the cost-per-year calculation often favours metal — but you need to be in the home long enough to capture that value.

Best for: Burnaby homeowners building or renovating for the long term, or those who want the lowest-maintenance option available and can absorb the higher initial cost.

Flat Membrane Systems: $8,000 – $18,000

Burnaby has a significant number of coach houses, secondary suites, and modern flat-roof additions. Torch-on SBS membrane — styrene-butadiene-styrene modified bitumen — is the standard for these applications on the West Coast. It handles standing water and freeze-thaw cycles well, which matters during Burnaby winters.

The cost range reflects roof size more than complexity. A small coach house roof might come in under $10,000. A larger flat section on a two-storey home with parapet walls and multiple drains sits higher. Proper drainage design is not optional if you want the membrane to last its full lifespan.

Best for: Burnaby homeowners with flat or low-slope roofs, coach houses, or secondary suites who need a proven, code-compliant membrane system.

What Affects Your Final Quote

Several variables move the number in either direction, regardless of material.

  • Roof size. Roofing is priced by the square — one square equals 100 square feet of roof surface. A larger footprint costs more in both materials and labour.
  • Pitch and complexity. Steeper roofs require safety equipment and slower installation. More valleys, hips, dormers, and penetrations add time.
  • Tear-off layers. One layer of existing shingles is standard. Two or more layers add disposal and labour costs.
  • Decking condition. Hidden rot only shows up after tear-off. Budget a contingency of $500 to $1,500 for potential decking repairs.
  • Gutters and flashing. If your gutters are original to the roof, replacing them at the same time saves labour. Stonnex installs seamless aluminum gutters as part of full replacement projects.
  • Skylights. Existing skylights need resealing or replacement during a roof job. VELUX skylight installation is available as part of the project.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a roof replacement cost in Burnaby in 2026?

It depends on the material and your home’s size. Architectural asphalt shingles typically run $9,000 to $16,000, cedar shake $18,000 to $32,000, standing seam metal $22,000 to $45,000 or more, and flat membrane systems $8,000 to $18,000. These ranges cover a typical detached Burnaby home between 1,500 and 2,500 square feet.

How long does a new roof last in Burnaby?

Architectural asphalt shingles last 25 to 30 years in BC’s wet coastal climate with proper installation. Cedar shake can reach 30 to 40 years with regular maintenance. Standing seam metal routinely lasts 50 years or more. Flat SBS membrane systems typically last 20 to 30 years depending on drainage and upkeep.

Does Burnaby’s climate affect roofing costs?

Yes. Over 1,200mm of annual rainfall accelerates moss growth and granule loss on asphalt and cedar roofs. Contractors factor in moss-resistant underlays, algae-resistant shingle coatings, and more thorough flashing work than you’d need in a drier climate. These add modest cost but extend the roof’s effective lifespan.

What is the cheapest roofing option for a Burnaby home?

Architectural asphalt shingles are the most affordable option for a full replacement, starting around $9,000 for a straightforward roof. They’re also the most widely installed material in Burnaby and carry manufacturer warranties up to 50 years depending on the product line.

Do I need a permit to replace my roof in Burnaby?

A like-for-like roof replacement in Burnaby generally doesn’t require a building permit, but structural changes or conversions from one roof type to another may. Your contractor should confirm permit requirements before work begins.

How do I get a roof replacement quote in Burnaby without a sales visit?

What should I ask a Burnaby roofing contractor before hiring?

Ask for the manufacturer warranty terms in writing, confirm WorkSafeBC registration — verifiable online — ask who your named project manager will be, and request a written scope of work before signing anything. A contractor who can answer all four questions clearly is worth taking seriously.
