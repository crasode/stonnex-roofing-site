Skylight Installation Vancouver: Costs, Styles, and What to Expect

Published July 15, 2026 · Stonnex Roofing

A skylight does more than add light. In Vancouver and across the Lower Mainland, where overcast skies dominate from October through March, a well-placed skylight can genuinely change how a room feels day to day. Done right, it also adds resale value and cuts your reliance on artificial lighting.

Done wrong, it becomes a source of leaks, condensation, and costly repairs.

This guide covers what skylight installation actually costs in Greater Vancouver in 2026, which styles suit BC homes best, and what the process looks like from start to finish.

Why Skylights Make Sense for Vancouver Homes

The Lower Mainland receives over 1,200mm of rain annually, and natural daylight is in short supply for a good chunk of the year. Skylights address both realities. They bring in diffused natural light through the grey months, and when properly specified, they handle heavy rain and coastal moisture without issue.

That phrase — “properly specified” — carries real weight here. A skylight that performs well in a dry climate won’t necessarily hold up on a North Vancouver or Burnaby roofline that sees sustained rainfall and significant temperature swings. The product choice, flashing method, and installation technique all matter.

Skylight Styles: Which Type Fits Your Home

Fixed Skylights

Fixed skylights don’t open. They’re the most common choice for living rooms, hallways, and stairwells where light is the goal and ventilation isn’t a concern. They’re also the most affordable option and carry a lower leak risk — no moving parts, no seals that degrade over time.

Venting Skylights

Venting skylights open, either manually with a pole or automatically via remote or rain sensor. They work well in kitchens, bathrooms, and home offices where airflow matters as much as light. VELUX offers both manual and solar-powered electric venting models, and the solar option requires no wiring — it charges directly from the skylight itself.

Tubular Skylights

Tubular skylights, sometimes called sun tunnels, use a small roof-mounted dome and a reflective tube to channel daylight into the room below. They’re a practical solution for interior rooms, closets, or hallways where a full skylight isn’t structurally feasible. The roof footprint is small, installation is faster, and the cost is lower.

Custom and Architectural Skylights

Larger custom units, pyramid shapes, and continuous ridge skylights show up on modern West Coast homes, coach houses, and laneway homes throughout Vancouver and the Fraser Valley. These require more planning, structural assessment, and precise flashing work — but the result is dramatic and durable when the installation is done properly.

Skylight costs in Greater Vancouver depend on the unit itself, the complexity of the roof, and what’s needed to finish the interior — drywall, trim, and light shaft framing.

Here are realistic 2026 ranges for residential installations across the Lower Mainland:

If your skylight installation is part of a larger roofing project, the per-unit cost often comes down — the crew is already on the roof, and scaffolding or staging is already in place. Pairing a skylight with a full roof replacement is one of the more cost-efficient ways to approach both.

What Affects the Final Price

Roof Pitch and Accessibility

Steeper roofs take longer and require more safety equipment. A 12/12 pitch in North Vancouver costs more to work on than a low-slope roof in Richmond or Delta. If your home needs scaffolding rather than roof jacks, that adds to the total.

Existing Framing Conditions

If the roof framing needs to be modified to fit the skylight opening, that work adds both time and cost. Older homes in Vancouver and Burnaby sometimes have non-standard rafter spacing that requires a structural header before anything else can happen.

Flashing Method

Flashing is where most skylight leaks start. Quality installation uses step flashing and a continuous sill pan — not sealant alone. On a cedar shake or standing seam metal roof, the flashing approach differs from what works on architectural asphalt shingles. The roofing material already on your home affects both the method and the cost.

Interior Finishing

The skylight unit is only part of the project. The light shaft below it — whether it’s a straight drop or an angled tunnel — needs to be framed, insulated, drywalled, and trimmed. This work is sometimes quoted separately by the roofing contractor or handed off to a general contractor. Clarify that scope before you accept any quote.

The Installation Process: What to Expect

Step 1: Assessment and Placement

Before any cutting happens, placement needs to be confirmed. The best location balances where you want light inside the room with what’s structurally feasible above. Your contractor should assess rafter spacing, any obstructions in the attic or ceiling cavity, and the roof’s orientation relative to the sun.

South-facing skylights deliver the most light year-round in BC. North-facing units provide consistent, diffused light without direct glare. East and west orientations work well for morning or afternoon light but can create heat gain in summer.

Step 2: Roof Opening and Framing

The roof deck is cut and the opening is framed with a header and trimmer rafters. This is the most structurally significant part of the job and should be done by an experienced crew — not rushed.

Step 3: Unit and Flashing Installation

The skylight unit is set into the opening and the flashing system is installed. On a quality installation, this includes a pre-formed sill pan, step flashing along the sides, and a counter-flashing cap at the head. The flashing is integrated into the surrounding roofing material, not simply laid over it.

Step 4: Interior Light Shaft

The light shaft is framed below the opening to direct light into the room. Depending on ceiling height and roof pitch, this may be a straight vertical shaft or an angled one. Insulation is fitted around the shaft to prevent condensation and heat loss — particularly important in the Fraser Valley, where temperature swings between seasons are more pronounced.

Step 5: Finishing and Testing

Once the interior is drywalled and trimmed, the installation is tested. A venting skylight should open and close smoothly. A solar-powered unit should respond to its remote. The flashing is inspected from the roof, and the interior is checked for any daylight gaps around the frame.

Common Questions Before You Commit

Will a skylight cause leaks?

A properly installed skylight with quality flashing won’t leak. Most skylight leaks traced back to roofing contractors come down to inadequate flashing, not product failure. That’s why the installation method and the installer’s certification matter as much as the unit itself.

Does a skylight affect my home insurance?

Adding a skylight doesn’t typically increase your home insurance premium, but you should notify your insurer. If the skylight is part of a broader roofing project, the updated inspection report from that work is useful documentation to keep on file.

How long does installation take?

A single standard skylight typically takes one to two days, including interior finishing. Larger or custom units, or installations on complex rooflines, may take longer.

What does skylight installation cost in Vancouver in 2026?

A standard fixed skylight installed on a residential roof in Greater Vancouver typically runs between $1,200 and $2,500 all-in. Venting skylights range from $1,800 to $4,500 depending on the model. Custom or architectural units can reach $10,000 or more. Interior finishing costs may be quoted separately.

How do I know if my roof can support a skylight?

What is a VELUX-certified installer and why does it matter?

VELUX certifies roofing contractors who meet its installation standards. Having a VELUX skylight installed by an uncertified contractor can void the product warranty. Stonnex is a VELUX-certified installer, which means your warranty stays fully intact.

Which skylight style works best for a Vancouver home?

For most homes in Vancouver and the Lower Mainland, a fixed or venting VELUX unit with a properly engineered flashing kit is the most reliable choice. Solar-powered venting skylights are popular in kitchens and bathrooms. Tubular skylights suit interior rooms where a full unit isn’t structurally practical.

Should I install a skylight at the same time as a roof replacement?

Yes, if you’re planning both. Combining the work reduces labour costs because the crew is already on the roof, and the flashing integration is cleaner when done alongside new roofing material. Installing a skylight on a roof near the end of its life means paying for flashing work twice.

How long does a skylight last on a BC coastal roof?

A quality VELUX skylight installed with proper flashing typically lasts 20 to 30 years on a BC coastal roofline. The flashing and surrounding roofing material are usually the first components to need attention, which is why installation quality matters as much as the unit itself.
