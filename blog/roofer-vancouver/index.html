Roofer Vancouver: How to Choose the Right Contractor in 2026

Published July 4, 2026 · Stonnex Roofing

Finding a roofer in Vancouver isn’t hard. Finding one you can actually trust with a $15,000 to $25,000 project — that’s a different question.

The Greater Vancouver market has no shortage of contractors, and most of them will answer your call. But the gap between a credentialed company with real accountability and a crew that disappears after the deposit clears is significant. Here’s exactly what to verify, what to ask, and what the warning signs look like before you sign anything.

Verify Credentials Before You Do Anything Else

In BC, roofing work falls under WorkSafeBC coverage requirements. Any contractor you hire must be registered and in good standing — you can confirm this directly on the WorkSafeBC website using their legal business name. If they can’t provide a registration number, stop there.

BBB Accredited Business status is worth checking too. It’s not a guarantee of quality, but it does mean the company has agreed to binding dispute resolution and has a reviewable complaint history. A contractor with a clean BBB profile is meaningfully more accountable than one without any.

Manufacturer certifications matter as well. IKO, GAF, and Malarkey all run installer certification programs, and certified installers are held to standards that protect your warranty. If a contractor installs GAF shingles but isn’t a certified GAF installer, your manufacturer warranty may be voided before the job is even finished.

Understand What a Real Warranty Actually Covers

Most Vancouver homeowners hear “25-year warranty” and assume it covers everything. It rarely does. Every roofing job comes with two separate warranties — the manufacturer’s material warranty and the contractor’s workmanship warranty — and one does not substitute for the other.

Material warranties from IKO, GAF, and Malarkey can extend up to 50 years on premium architectural shingle systems, but only when a certified contractor installs them to spec. If the installation is off-spec, the warranty can be voided entirely.

Workmanship warranties cover the contractor’s installation quality, typically for two to ten years depending on the company. Ask for both warranties in writing before work begins, and confirm the workmanship warranty is transferable if you sell. In a market where home sales are common, that transferability is a real asset.

Ask Who Will Be Managing Your Project

This is the question most homeowners forget, and it matters more than almost anything else on this list.

On many roofing jobs, the person who sold you the project and the crew that shows up on day one have never spoken. You end up managing communication between an office and a subcontracted crew, chasing updates, and hoping the scope you agreed to is what actually gets installed. That’s how surprises happen.

A named project manager assigned specifically to your job changes that dynamic. One person knows your address, your scope, your materials, and your timeline — and you have someone to call if something looks wrong on day two. Ask any contractor you’re evaluating: “Who is my named project manager, and how do I reach them directly?”

If the answer is vague, that tells you something.

Compare Quotes the Right Way

Getting three quotes is standard advice. Comparing them is harder than it sounds. Two quotes for a “complete roof replacement” in Vancouver can differ by $8,000 and still be describing different scopes of work.

When you receive a quote, confirm it specifies: the exact material being installed (manufacturer, product line, and shingle grade); whether the existing roof will be torn off or overlaid; how many layers are currently on the roof; what happens if the decking underneath is damaged or rotten; warranty terms for both materials and workmanship; and a project start date and estimated completion timeline.

A quote that doesn’t answer all of these isn’t a real quote. It’s an estimate that gives the contractor room to add costs once the job is underway.

Red Flags Worth Knowing

Most roofing problems in Vancouver don’t come from outright fraud. They come from contractors who are underqualified, underinsured, or overextended. Watch for these warning signs:

No WorkSafeBC number. Non-negotiable. If a worker is injured on your property and the contractor isn’t registered, you may carry the liability.

Quote delivered verbally, not in writing. A verbal quote isn’t enforceable. Get everything in a written document.

Large deposit required upfront. A reasonable deposit is 10 to 20 percent. Fifty percent or more before work begins is a red flag.

No physical business address. A contractor operating only from a mobile number and a Facebook page has no fixed accountability.

Pressure to decide immediately. Any contractor who tells you the price is only good today is using a sales tactic. A reputable Vancouver roofing company will hold a written quote for a reasonable period — no pressure required.

The Free Inspection Is Not a Sales Visit

Many homeowners avoid booking a roof inspection because they assume it’s a pretext for a pitch. With a reputable contractor, it shouldn’t be.

A proper free inspection produces a written report with photographs documenting your roof’s current condition — detailed enough to be accepted by your insurer or lender. It should tell you whether your roof is near the end of its life, what specific issues exist, and what your options are. You’re under no obligation to proceed with any work.

If you’re seeing signs you need a new roof, or preparing for an insurance renewal or home sale, that written report has real value regardless of what you decide to do next. Stonnex provides this at no cost, with optional drone imagery and thermal assessment for a more complete picture.

How Vancouver’s Climate Should Influence Your Decision

Over 1,200mm of rain falls across the Lower Mainland each year. Add the salt air in coastal areas like West Vancouver and North Vancouver, and the moss and algae growth common across Surrey, Burnaby, and Coquitlam, and you have a climate that’s genuinely hard on roofing materials.

This matters when choosing a contractor because not every roofer has meaningful experience with the materials that actually perform well here. Cedar shake, standing seam metal, and SBS-modified architectural shingles all behave differently in our wet coastal climate than they do in drier markets. A contractor who primarily works inland may not account for the ventilation and moisture management details that determine whether a roof lasts 15 years or 30.

Ask any contractor you’re evaluating how many roofs they’ve replaced in Vancouver specifically, and what materials they recommend for homes in your neighbourhood. The answer will tell you a lot. A repair and a full replacement are very different in cost, and the right answer depends on the age of your roof, the condition of the decking, and how much useful life remains.

FAQs

What credentials should a Vancouver roofer have?
At minimum: WorkSafeBC registration, general liability insurance, and BBB Accredited Business status. Manufacturer certifications from IKO, GAF, or Malarkey are also important — they protect your material warranty and confirm the contractor meets installation standards.

How do I verify a roofer is WorkSafeBC registered in BC?
Search the WorkSafeBC website directly using the contractor’s legal business name. Registration is public and searchable. Ask for their registration number and confirm it yourself before signing anything.

What is a reasonable deposit for a roofing job in Vancouver?
Ten to 20 percent of the total project cost is standard. Any contractor asking for 50 percent or more upfront before work begins is outside the normal range and warrants caution.

What does a free roof inspection actually include?
A proper inspection produces a written report with photographs documenting your roof’s current condition. It should identify specific issues, estimate remaining useful life, and be detailed enough for a BC insurer or lender to accept. You’re under no obligation to proceed with any work after receiving it.

Are instant online roofing quotes accurate?
Address-based tools that pull real manufacturer pricing give you a reliable ballpark for a standard replacement. They can’t account for hidden rot, unusual geometry, or existing layers until an in-person inspection confirms those details. Use the instant quote to establish a reasonable cost range, then confirm the final number through a written in-person assessment.

Why does a named project manager matter on a roofing job?
It means one person is accountable for your project from start to finish. Without that, communication tends to break down between the sales team and the installation crew. A named PM gives you a direct contact for questions, scope changes, and anything that comes up during the job.

How do I compare roofing quotes in Vancouver when they look different?
Ask each contractor to specify the exact material (manufacturer, product line, and grade), whether the existing roof is being torn off or overlaid, how decking damage is handled and priced, and what both the material and workmanship warranties cover. Quotes that don’t answer these questions aren’t comparable — regardless of the headline number.
